ATA 12–20 Servicing - Scheduled Servicing - Maintenance Practices - Exterior Cleaning 
 
When cleaning the exterior of the aircraft it is very important to follow the procedures outlined in 
chapter 12 of the Bonanza / Baron Maintenance Manual. There have been many problems 
associated with improper washing techniques especially with the use of high-pressure washers. 
High pressure washers are not recommended at all for cleaning the aircraft exterior including 
the landing gear and engine components. High-pressure water washes away wheel bearing 
grease, removes lubricants and paint and will cause water and foreign matter to become 
trapped in seams, hinges, bushings and bearings. Overspray in the avionics compartment can 
cause serious corrosion problems and damage to electrical equipment.  It is very important to 
keep in mind the different types of environment your aircraft is subjected to and lubrication 
periods may have to be performed more often than the specified interval (refer to chapter 12 of 
your Maintenance Manual). The following procedure is to be used as guide for proper aircraft 
wash and lubrication of a Bonanza / Baron Aircraft. 
 
Preparation for cleaning 
 
1. Refer to chapter 12 of the Maintenance Manual; lubricate the landing gear, flight control 
hinges and gear doors prior to washing aircraft. This will help prevent water from penetrating 
into the lube joints. 
 
NOTE: Prior to lubricating any components or parts, remove all foreign matter from joints, 
fittings and bearing surfaces. A soft cloth dampened with naphtha is preferable since it will not 
damage the paint surface. 
 
2. Lower flaps to full down position. 
 
3. Engage parking brake and control locks. 
 
4. Close doors and emergency openings. 
 
5. Check moisture / condensation drain holes. Ensure all drain holes are clear by carefully 
inserting a probe such as a pipe cleaner. 
 
6. To protect against water and cleaning solution intrusion tape the following: 
 
CAUTION: Ensure that static vents are not fouled by tape adhesive transfer. Cover 
static vents with a properly sized disc of suitable material like a plastic cap then 
apply masking tape. Also a suitable “flag” on the tape will serve as a reminder to 
remove after the completion of washing.

7. Wipe exposed chrome area of the shock struts with MIL-H-5606 hydraulic fluid. 
 
8. Start by washing the landing gear and letting the cleaning solution soak a few minutes

9.  Wash the aircraft exterior starting with the under side center and work toward the          
      nose, tail, and wing tips. Wash the top of aircraft starting top center and work toward        
      the nose, tail, and wing tips. To prevent streaking, start at the lower surfaces working 
      upward and out. 
 
10.  Rinse away the loosened soil and cleaner with a stream of water avoiding direct        
       contact with lubrication joints. Dry the cleaned surface with a chamois to prevent  
       water spots. 
 
11. REMOVE all covers and masking material installed during preparation for cleaning. 
 
12.  Check moisture and condensation drain holes by carefully inserting a probe such as a 
        Pipe cleaner to assure that all drains are clear and no water accumulation has  
        occurred. 
 
12. Re-lubricate items in step one.

ATA 32 Landing Gear- Bonanza / Baron Landing Gear Actuator 
 
Hawker Beechcraft Corporation no longer recommends the use of Kit Number 95-8100-0001, 
KIT - ACTUATOR ASSY, SEAL REPLACEMENT. Field reports indicated that this kit may not 
yield the desired result, i.e., a landing gear actuator that does not leak oil. HBC stopped the 
production of P/N 95-810017-29 and reinstated the 95-810017-27 actuator. If you have an 
existing -29 actuator or an earlier actuator modified by the incorporation of Kit No. 95-8100-0001 
it may be reworked by using the improved sealing method; remove the -31, -33 and (2) -35 
gaskets and add sealant material as outlined below. This should reduce the possibility of an oil 
leak developing in service. 
 
 
Alternate sealing method for 95-810017 series landing gear retract actuators –  
 
Alternate sealing method between housings: Permatex #2 and silk thread per Teledyne 
Continental Motors, Inc. P/N 641543 (or equivalent), installed between 6 screws as  shown on 
Figure 1, approximately 15 inch thread length required.

ATA 61 PROPELLER - Supplemental Installation Information for Hartzell Propeller 
 
 
 
 
 
Installation procedure for the Hartzell three blade “F” type flange propellers may be found in 
Section 3, Installation and Removal (61-00-15) of the current version of Hartzell Propeller 
Owner’s Manual, No. 115N. This article contains supplemental information to clarify sequence 
torquing procedure on the Bonanza and Baron Series aircraft. Use torque sequence “A” above 
for the initial and secondary torquing procedure. Use torque sequence “B” above for final torque. 
                                   
                         
CAUTION 
IMPROPER OR CARELESS INSTALLATION OF THE PROPELLER MAY 
DAMAGE THE HUB OR O-RING GROOVE 
 
The propeller must be installed with great care on the engine crankshaft flange.  Aligning of the 
dowel pins must be straight so as to not damage the propeller hub or o-ring or o-ring groove before 
starting the tightening or torquing procedure. Do not use the propeller mounting hardware to draw 
the dowel pins into position as this can result in damage to the propeller hub, o-ring, or o-ring 
groove. The propeller must be flush with engine prop flange before tightening or torquing 
mounting hardware.
