# SOP #1 — Interior Maintenance Cleaning

**Service:** Interior Maintenance Cleaning (Phase 1 + Phase 2)  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-02-Interior-Maintenance-Cleaning.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

## 1. Purpose

Passengers focus on the interior more than the exterior. They are paying for a comfortable, pleasant experience from the moment they step on board — and most are accustomed to luxury accommodations and notice every detail. A clean interior also reflects on the pilot, the charter operator, and the crew. A dirty interior leads directly to complaints.

Interior maintenance cleaning is a two-phase service performed in sequence:

- Phase 1 — Hard surfaces, windows, monitors, galley, cockpit, lavatory
- Phase 2 — Vacuuming all carpets, fabric, and crevices

Phase 1 always comes before Phase 2. Dust knocked free during hard-surface cleaning settles down to be vacuumed up afterward — vacuuming first wastes the effort.

Interior maintenance cleaning is the foundation for any deeper interior service (deep leather clean, carpet extraction, full interior detail).

Important: Disinfectants must follow the aircraft manufacturer's approved product list. Never fog the interior — the FAA advises against fogging because aerosolized chemicals enter electronics, ducting, and panel cavities where they cannot be flushed out.

## 3. Tools & Equipment

### Vacuum

- Phase 1 (dusting) — handheld cordless vacuum (Fanttik V8 APEX or equivalent). Use the crevice tool on the small hose for everything.
- Phase 2 (full vacuuming) — MetroVac Super Vac N Blaster (or equivalent commercial detailing vacuum/blower combo). Use a combination of:
- Stand-up wand with boar's-hair brush attachment on the end — main carpet and floor passes
- Crevice tool — tight areas, seat tracks, floor seams, between seats
- On smaller aircraft, the crevice and claw attachments alone are enough — the wand is for jet/larger cabins.

### Towels (color-coded and isolated)

- Multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber — primary interior towel. Used folded into quarters: spray Quick Turn on one side, wipe; flip to fresh side, wipe again. Dedicated towel per category.
- Cotton terry towels — entry step drying, oil cleanup

Towel categories — never share between them:

- Cabin hard surfaces
- Cabin windows
- Cabin monitors
- Cockpit hard surfaces
- Cockpit windows
- HUD (new each cockpit)
- Each cockpit monitor (new towel per monitor)
- Lavatory (3 per fixture — see Phase 1 Lav Procedure)

### Supplies

- 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape
- Moderately stiff-bristled brush — entry steps
- Soft-bristled brush — interior dusting (used together with vacuum nozzle nearby)
- Handheld steamer — entry steps (neutralizes Carbon Blitz residue so steps aren't slippery)
- Shiny Jets "Cockpit Cleaned — Please Verify Switches and Breakers" tag (placed on yoke or pedestal after cockpit service)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access

Pre-staging: All tools must be cleaned of exterior contamination (oil, grease, polish residue, soot) before entering the aircraft.

## 4. PPE

PPE must meet recognized international occupational safety standards.

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166 (splash rated for disinfectants)
- Hand protection: Nitrile chemical-resistant gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1 and EN ISO 374-5. Disposable nitrile minimum 6 mil. Fresh gloves before entering the lavatory; discard after lav work — never carry lav contamination back into the cabin.
- Foot protection: Clean, non-marking shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413. Shoe covers are required in the aircraft to avoid tracking exterior contamination onto carpets. Replace shoe covers any time they pick up debris.
- Clothing: Clean uniform. No belt buckles, watches, rings, or hard accessories that could scratch interior surfaces. Long sleeves recommended.

## 5. Pre-Service Walkaround (Mandatory)

### Shiny Jets CRM — Pre-Service Photo Upload

Before the walkaround, open the Shiny Jets CRM on your mobile device:

1. Navigate to the Customer → select the Aircraft record (by tail number).
2. Open the active Job for today's service.
3. Upload pre-service photos to the aircraft record: - Overall cabin condition (forward, mid, aft) - Cockpit condition (both seats, panels, floor) - Galley condition (counters, sinks, cabinetry, coolers) - Lavatory condition (vanity, sink, toilet, mirrors, floor) - Each seat (close-up of any stains, wear, scuffs) - Carpet (close-up of any stains, matting, debris) - Leather and trim (close-up of any wear, cracking, ink, dye transfer) - Windows and monitors (close-up of any scratches or smudging baseline) - Any personal items visible in the cabin (so the crew is not blamed for moving them — and so items can be returned to their exact position). Photograph what is in plain view; do not open cabinets, drawers, or bags to inventory contents unless the customer has specifically asked for that level of cleaning.
4. Tag each photo with the affected location (e.g., "seat 3A right armrest," "galley aft cabinet").
5. Confirm upload completed before moving to the walkaround.

These photos protect both the customer and the crew from disputes over pre-existing damage and missing items.

### Walkaround

Conducted before unloading any equipment. Walk the interior in the same direction every time so it becomes muscle memory.

### Inspect and document

- Leather wear, cracking, dye transfer, ink stains
- Carpet stains, burns, tears, heavy matting
- Trim damage (wood veneer chips, plastic cracks, scuffed plating)
- Window scratches, hazing, delamination
- Monitor screen scratches or burn-in
- Galley spills, broken fixtures, mildew in sink areas
- Lavatory: cracked vanity, broken faucet, toilet condition, mirror damage
- Any odor sources (food, smoke, biological, mildew)
- Loose or damaged interior fasteners
- Inoperative cabin or cockpit lights
- Personal items, paperwork, headsets, or equipment to be photographed and left in place

Get door operation instructions from the pilot or POC before any door is touched. Aircraft cabin doors are not standardized — wrong handling can damage door seals, hinges, or hydraulic struts.

If damage is found: Stop. Notify pilot, owner, or mechanic (your designated POC) before starting work.

## 6. Aircraft Preparation

1. Confirm the detailer doing interior work has not polished brightwork that day. Metal polish residue rides on hands, gloves, sleeves, and tools and transfers to interior surfaces — leather, headliner, trim. If a detailer polished brightwork earlier the same day, swap them onto a different aircraft or have them fully change clothes and decontaminate before entering the cabin.
2. Confirm exterior maintenance work is complete or not happening simultaneously. Avoid cross-contamination.
3. Wipe down all tools (vacuum hoses, attachments, towel bins) outside the aircraft before bringing them in.
4. Place dedicated bins at the door for: clean towels, dirty towels (cabin), dirty towels (lav).
5. Tape any door frames or trim where the vacuum hose will rub during use (2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape, removed at end of shift — never left overnight).
6. On low-wing aircraft, tape the trailing edge of the wing at the entry side. Crew members entering and exiting drag hoses, bottles, towel bins, and feet across the wing trailing edge near the door — abrasion here damages the paint on the trailing edge. Apply 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape along the entire trailing edge that traffic will cross. Remove at end of shift.

7. Confirm cabin lighting is set to the brightest interior mode. If the aircraft is on ground power, use cabin lights. If ground power is not available, work by headlamp — do not run cabin lighting off the aircraft battery for extended periods.
8. Use a headlamp for inspection in dark areas regardless of cabin lighting (under seats, under tables, footwells, panel gaps).

Note: Entry step cleaning happens AFTER all interior services are complete — see Section 11. Do not pre-clean the steps before working the interior; crew will track contamination onto freshly cleaned steps during the job.

## 7. Phase 1 — Hard Surfaces, Windows, Monitors

Phase 1 universal rules:

1. Dust and vacuum at the same time as you wipe. As you brush dust off any horizontal hard surface with the soft-bristled brush, hold the vacuum nozzle in the vicinity of the brush to capture the dust that floats up — do not touch the nozzle to the hard surface. The brush agitates, the vacuum captures. Then immediately wipe that same area with Quick Turn before moving on.
2. Work one area at a time. Stand in one position, dust + vacuum the area within reach, wipe it down with Quick Turn, then transition to the next area. Do not dust the whole cabin first and then come back to wipe.
3. Spray onto the towel, never onto the surface. Especially near electrical panels, switches, or screens.
4. Fold towels twice (into quarters). Spray Quick Turn on one side and wipe. When that side loads up, flip the towel and wipe again on the fresh side. Four working surfaces per towel.

Surfaces wiped with Quick Turn:

- Tables, cabinetry, monitors/TVs, windows (interior), seat belt buckles and latches, headrest lifters, seat adjustment levers, visors, sinks, gauges (the bezel, not the glass face), painted and chrome trim parts (Quick Turn is safe on both), cup holders, magazine racks.

Surfaces handled differently:

- Light leather smudges and fingerprints — damp multi-purpose microfiber, light pressure only. Anything beyond a smudge is a leather cleaning service, not maintenance.
- Matte finishes, plastic, pleather — wiping these to remove dirt or residue is a detail-level service, not maintenance. Dust and vacuum only.
- Oil or grease residue on hard surfaces where it shouldn't be — wipe with Oil Delete on a terry towel, then follow with Quick Turn. Do not blast or strip oil out of intentional joints, hinges, or grease points — only clean surfaces where oil or grease has migrated to a place it doesn't belong.
- Headliner — do not wipe the cabin headliner ever unless the customer has specifically asked. Headliners are fragile, water-stain easily, and discolor permanently.
- Yokes — not part of maintenance cleaning. Floor yokes only: vacuum the rubber boot at the base. Do not wipe the yoke itself.

Phase 1 procedures differ for jets / larger aircraft versus smaller aircraft (single/twin piston). Use the section that matches the aircraft you are working on.

Phase 1 — Jets and Larger Aircraft Flow: Enter the cabin. Work down the co-pilot side all the way to the back. Cross over at the rear and work out the pilot side back to the front. Galley, then cockpit, then lavatory.

This flow keeps you moving in a single continuous path and prevents jumping back and forth across the cabin.

### Step 1 — Co-Pilot Side, Front to Back (Section by Section)

Stand in the first section just inside the entry on the co-pilot side. Dust + vacuum the area within reach, then immediately wipe that area down, then take one step aft and repeat.

For each section, dust + vacuum:

- Horizontal hard surfaces within reach — cabinetry tops, table tops, side ledges
- Cup holders (every one — crumbs and dried liquid collect in the bottom)
- Magazine rack interior, air vent louvers, USB and charger recesses
- Seat belt buckle and latch housings, seat adjustment lever recesses, headrest lifters
- Seam between cushion and armrest, between cushion and side bolster

Then wipe with Quick Turn (towel folded into quarters, fresh side per pass):

- Cabinetry faces, table tops, side panels
- Seat belt buckles and latches, headrest lifters, seat adjustment levers
- Cup holders, magazine racks, USB/charger recesses
- Light leather smudges only — damp multi-purpose microfiber, no chemical

Move one section aft and repeat until you reach the rear cabin.

### Step 2 — Windows and Monitors (Co-Pilot Side, As You Go)

Cabin interior windows and monitors do not have ground-in dirt — only soda marks, forehead smudges, and finger smudges. Use a multi-purpose microfiber 16×16, folded into quarters.

- Spray Quick Turn on one side of the folded towel. Wipe the window or monitor.
- Flip to the fresh side and wipe again until clear.
- Use a dedicated towel for monitors and a separate dedicated towel for windows — do not share.
- Do not press hard on monitors. LCDs and OLEDs damage under pressure.

Work the windows and monitors in the same section as Step 1 so you only stand in each section once.

### Step 3 — Cross Over at the Rear, Work Out the Pilot Side

Once you reach the back of the cabin on the co-pilot side, cross over to the pilot side and work forward, section by section, repeating Steps 1 and 2 in reverse direction.

### Step 4 — Galley

Stand in the galley. Dust + vacuum:

- Counter seams, drawer slides, cabinet edges
- Sink basin corners, faucet base
- Coffee maker / microwave / oven exterior seams (soft brush only, no liquid near control panels)

Then wipe with Quick Turn:

- Counters, cabinet faces, drawer fronts
- Sink basin (then dry — no standing water)
- Coffee maker, microwave, oven exteriors (towel only — do not spray near control panels)
- Faucet, drain area, latches, handles

### Step 5 — Cockpit

Use dedicated cockpit towels — never cabin or lav towels.

Dust + vacuum:

- Around panel edges, breaker rows, switch seams, knob bases. The brush + vacuum combo pulls dust out of these areas without touching the panel itself.
- Pedestal exterior seams, side ledge edges, chart pocket interior
- Pilot and co-pilot seat creases, belt buckle housings, footwell seams

Then wipe with Quick Turn (cockpit towel folded into quarters):

- Glareshield, sun visors, throttle quadrant exterior, center pedestal exterior, side ledges, chart pockets
- Pilot and co-pilot seat hard surfaces — buckle, latch, adjustment levers (these build up with dust and food crumbs and need a wipedown), headrest lifter
- Light leather smudges on the cockpit seats only — damp multi-purpose microfiber

Separate towels in the cockpit:

- HUD — dedicated HUD towel, brand new for each cockpit
- Each monitor — dedicated monitor towel, brand new for each monitor
- Cockpit windows — dedicated cockpit window towel, separate from cabin window towel

Critical cockpit rules:

- Do not spray anywhere on the panel itself. Spray onto the towel, away from the aircraft.
- Do not operate any switch, knob, breaker, or trim wheel. If something is in the way, ask the pilot to move it before continuing.
- If you hear a switch or breaker accidentally flip, stop and notify the pilot immediately. Do not attempt to reset it yourself.
- Leave a Shiny Jets "Cockpit Cleaned — Please Verify Switches and Breakers" tag on the yoke or center pedestal before you leave the cockpit. The pilot must run their own switch/breaker check before any flight following interior service.

### Step 6 — Lavatory

See Phase 1 — Lavatory Procedure below.

Phase 1 — Smaller Aircraft (Single/Twin Piston) Smaller aircraft cabins are tight. Two rules govern everything in this section:

1. Never put a knee on any cushion. The pressure of a knee on aircraft leather or fabric breaks down stitching and stretches material. Sit in each seat to clean it.
2. Protect every hard surface from your hoses, bottles, and bins. In a tight cabin, vacuum hoses and bottle bottoms drag across cabinetry, side panels, and instrument bezels. Watch every move.

Scope note — hard surfaces in pistons: There is very little hard-surface wiping in a piston. The hard surfaces are typically: monitors, interior windows, visors, gauges (the bezel, not the glass face), seat belt buckles and latches, seat adjustment levers, headrest lifters, and any makeshift cup holders or pilot map/ storage pockets. A Baron and similar may have a small table — wipe that with Quick Turn. Outside of those, the work is dusting, vacuuming, and light leather smudge cleanup — not wiping every surface.

### Step 1 — Pre-Position

1. Do not move the front seats forward or aft on the track unless the pilot has shown you how and the customer is okay with it. Seat tracks on older small aircraft are tricky, fragile, and easy to damage. If it doesn't move easily, do not force anything. Vacuum under the seat from where it sits.
2. If the customer or pilot has explicitly approved moving the seats and shown you the release, fine — but the default is leave them where they are.
3. If you have been checked out by the pilot on the specific aircraft's seat operation, follow what they showed you.
4. Place protective material (clean microfiber or soft mat) on any hard surface where a hose or bin will need to rest.

### Step 2 — Forward Panel and Visors

1. Dust + vacuum around the glareshield seam, panel edges, visor mounting points, and chart pocket.
2. Wipe the glareshield, visors, gauge bezels, and any small painted or chrome trim with Quick Turn on a cockpit-dedicated towel folded into quarters.
3. Do not spray on the panel itself. Spray onto the towel away from the aircraft.
4. Do not operate any switch, knob, or breaker. Same cockpit rules as jet — if something flips, notify the pilot. Leave the Shiny Jets verification tag.

### Step 3 — Each Seat, In Sequence

For each seat — sit in it to clean it:

Dust + vacuum:

- Seat belt buckle housing, latch mechanism, seat creases
- Seat adjustment lever recess and any seat-mounted controls
- Cup holder or makeshift map/storage pocket on that side
- Door pocket on that side

Then sit and wipe with Quick Turn:

- Seat belt buckle and latch
- Seat adjustment lever (these build up with dust and food crumbs — clean every time)
- Headrest lifter
- Light leather smudges with a damp multi-purpose microfiber (no chemical, light pressure)

Do not lean your weight into the seat back while wiping. Reach in, wipe, lean back out.

### Step 4 — Windows and Monitors

Use multi-purpose 16×16 microfiber towels, folded into quarters. No Dreadnaught towels inside the aircraft.

- Dedicated towel for windows, dedicated towel for monitors. Do not share.
- Spray Quick Turn on one side. Wipe. Flip to fresh side. Wipe again.
- Interior windows only have light smudges (soda marks, finger and forehead smudges) — no ground-in dirt.

### Step 5 — Floor Yoke (If Equipped)

If the aircraft has a floor yoke, vacuum the rubber boot at the base of the yoke. Do not wipe the yoke itself. The boot collects dirt and crumbs.

### Step 6 — Lavatory (If Equipped)

Some larger pistons have a small lavatory. If so, follow the Phase 1 — Lavatory Procedure below.

Phase 1 — Lavatory Procedure (Both Aircraft Categories) The lavatory is cleaned last because it is the dirtiest area on the aircraft. Put on fresh gloves before entering. Discard them at the lav door when finished.

Lavatory cleaning uses three dedicated towels per fixture — never one set for the whole lav. This prevents cross-contamination between the faucet, the toilet seat, and the toilet bowl, which are three different bacterial environments.

Critical rule: never let disinfectant dry on a surface. Always wipe disinfectant off before it evaporates. Dried disinfectant streaks, hazes mirrors and chrome, and over time damages finishes.

Step A — Other Hard Surfaces First (Vanity, Cabinetry, Mirrors, Door, Switches)

1. Use Quick Turn by default for vanity tops, cabinetry, mirrors, door interior, and light switches. Quick Turn is safer on sensitive finishes that some disinfectants can damage.
2. If you do use disinfectant on these surfaces and it leaves streaks, hit each affected surface with a fourth towel and Quick Turn until clear.
3. Never let disinfectant dwell to dry.

Step B — Faucet and Sink (3 Towels, Dedicated)

1. Towel 1 — Clean wipe. Wipe the faucet, then the sink basin.
2. Towel 2 — Disinfectant. Saturate with approved aviation disinfectant. Wipe faucet, then sink. Watch the surface and move to Step 3 before evaporation.
3. Towel 3 — Dry wipe. Dry completely. Buff out streaking.

Discard all three towels. None of these towels touches anything else.

Step C — Toilet Seat Area (3 Towels, Dedicated)

1. Towel 1 — Clean wipe. Wipe lid (top and underside), seat (top and underside), hinges, base exterior.
2. Towel 2 — Disinfectant. Saturate, wipe in the same order. Do not let it dry — move to Step 3 before evaporation.
3. Towel 3 — Dry wipe. Dry completely.

Discard all three towels. None of these towels touches anything else.

Step D — Toilet Bowl (3 Towels, Dedicated)

1. Towel 1 — Clean wipe. Wipe bowl interior.
2. Towel 2 — Disinfectant. Saturate, wipe. Do not let it dry.
3. Towel 3 — Dry wipe. Dry the bowl interior.

Discard all three towels.

Step E — Exit the Lavatory

Remove gloves at the lav door and discard with the lav towels. Replace with fresh gloves before re-entering the cabin. Do not carry any lav towel, glove, or bottle back into the cabin for any reason.

## 8. Phase 2 — Vacuuming

Phase 2 starts after Phase 1 is fully complete and any disturbed dust has settled.

Vacuum tooling:

- Jets (cabin you can stand up in): stand-up wand with boar's-hair brush attachment for carpet and floor, crevice tool for tight areas (between seats, seat tracks, floor seams).

- Smaller aircraft (cabin you cannot stand up in): crevice tool and claw tool. The stand-up wand does not fit.

Phase 2 — Jets and Larger Aircraft Same flow as Phase 1: enter, work down the co-pilot side to the back, cross over, work out the pilot side back to the front. Galley last in the main cabin sequence.

For each section as you work:

1. Stand-up wand with boar's-hair brush on the carpet and floor within reach.
2. Crevice tool for tight areas — between cushions, between seat and side panel, footwell corners, around table bases, seat tracks.
3. Do not vacuum drawers or cabinet interiors. Do vacuum the luggage area and coat closet.

After the cabin, do the lavatory floor (claw or crevice for tight corners around the toilet base and door seams), then the cockpit floor (crevice tool for floor seams; do not use the claw on the cockpit floor — it can catch on wiring and floor screws).

Phase 2 — Smaller Aircraft (Single/Twin Piston) Use crevice and claw tools. Vacuum in whatever flow works best for the aircraft — generally:

## 1. Luggage area first (so contamination from luggage doesn't get carried forward)

## 2. Back seats

## 3. Front seats (pilot and co-pilot positions)

## 4. Floor yoke boot (if equipped)

After Phase 2 Take the vacuum outside the aircraft and leave it there. Do not walk back through the cabin with the vacuum off. Instead, walk through with your phone for the post-service CRM photo upload and final visual check (see Section 9). Use a headlamp during this inspection pass.

## 9. Entry Step Cleaning (After All Interior Services Complete)

Entry steps are cleaned LAST — after every interior service on the aircraft is fully complete. Crew traffic during interior work, leather cleaning, carpet cleaning, or any other interior service will track contamination onto freshly cleaned steps and undo the work. The steps come at the very end.

If a service is run separately on a different day (for example, carpet cleaning later in the week), the entry steps are cleaned again at the end of that visit.

Procedure For each step:

1. Spray Fly Shiny Carbon Blitz onto the step.
2. Agitate with a moderately stiff-bristled brush to lift exhaust soot and ground-in dirt.
3. Wipe the step clean with a cotton terry towel.
4. Steam the step with the handheld steamer to remove all Carbon Blitz residue. Residue left behind makes the step slippery — this is a fall hazard.
5. Dry the step with a fresh cotton terry towel.

Repeat for every step in sequence (bottom to top, so you finish closest to the door).

Maintenance-Only Job If today's job is interior maintenance cleaning only (no leather, no carpet, no other interior services), steps are still cleaned and steamed at the end of the interior work, following the same procedure.

## 10. Quality Control Walkaround

Walk the interior front-to-back and verify:

- All hard surfaces wiped (no fingerprints, smudges, dust film)
- Windows and monitors clear from all viewing angles, no streaks
- Galley sink dry, no standing water
- Cockpit clean, no overspray on any panel, "Cockpit Cleaned" tag in place on yoke or pedestal
- Lavatory clean, disinfected, dry — no chemical residue, no odor
- All carpet vacuumed including under tables and between seats
- Entry steps clean, dry, not slippery
- All tape removed, no adhesive residue
- All personal items returned to exact photographed position
- All tools, towels, and bins removed from the aircraft
- Cabin lighting returned to the setting requested by the pilot (or off)
- All doors and compartments closed

## 11. Critical Don'ts

- Don't start any service before the pre-service CRM photo upload and walkaround.
- Don't leave the aircraft before the post-service CRM upload is complete.
- Don't fog the interior — the FAA advises against it and aerosolized chemicals enter electronics and ducting that cannot be flushed.
- Don't spray any cleaner directly onto a panel, switch, breaker, screen, gauge, or instrument. Always onto the towel.
- Don't use ammonia-based cleaners on plastic trim or screens — they cause clouding and cracking.
- Don't use silicone-containing products — residue prevents future paint, touch-up, and adhesion of other treatments.
- Don't bring exterior-contaminated tools (oil, grease, brightwork polish, soot) into the aircraft.
- Don't do interior work the same day you've polished brightwork. Metal polish residue rides on hands, clothes, and tools and transfers into the cabin. Either change detailers or fully decontaminate.
- Don't move any personal item without photographing it first and returning it to the exact position.
- Don't let disinfectant dry on any surface. Always wipe before evaporation.
- Don't use the same towel for the lavatory and any other area of the aircraft.
- Don't cross-contaminate inside the lavatory. Faucet/sink towels, toilet seat towels, and toilet bowl towels stay with their assigned fixture.
- Don't use the same towel for the cockpit and the cabin.
- Don't share towels between windows and monitors, or between the HUD and any other surface.
- Don't put a knee on any cushion in a smaller aircraft — sit in each seat to clean it.
- Don't force seat tracks on smaller aircraft. If a seat doesn't move easily, leave it and vacuum from where it sits. Older tracks are fragile.
- Don't drag hoses, bottles, or tools across hard surfaces in a tight cabin.
- Don't oversaturate leather, suede, alcantara, or fabric — a damp towel is correct, a wet one is wrong.
- Don't wipe matte finishes, plastic, or pleather to remove residue. That's detail work, not maintenance.
- Don't wipe the cabin headliner unless the customer has specifically asked for it.

- Don't wipe the yoke. If it's a floor yoke, vacuum the rubber boot at the base — that's it.
- Don't blast oil out of joints, hinges, or grease points with Oil Delete. Oil Delete is for hard surfaces where oil has migrated to a place it shouldn't be.
- Don't operate any switch, breaker, knob, trim wheel, or control in the cockpit.
- Don't ignore a switch or breaker that flipped accidentally. Stop and notify the pilot immediately.
- Don't leave the cockpit without placing the "Cockpit Cleaned — Please Verify Switches and Breakers" tag on the yoke or pedestal.
- Don't touch the vacuum nozzle directly to a hard surface. The brush agitates, the vacuum captures dust nearby.
- Don't walk back through the cabin with the vacuum off after Phase 2. Take the vacuum outside, then do the final inspection pass with phone and headlamp only.
- Don't vacuum drawers or cabinets. Do vacuum luggage area and coat closet.
- Don't leave tape on any surface overnight — adhesive transfer becomes permanent.
- Don't use the claw attachment on the cockpit floor — it can catch on wiring and floor screws.
- Don't clean entry steps before interior work is complete — crew traffic will recontaminate them. Steps come last.
- Don't leave Carbon Blitz residue on entry steps. Steam neutralizes it. Residue makes the step slippery.
