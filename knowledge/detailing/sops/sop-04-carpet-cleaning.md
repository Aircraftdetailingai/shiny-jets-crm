# SOP #4 — Carpet Cleaning

**Service:** Wool & Natural Fiber Carpet Extraction, Stain Removal, Drying  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Version:** June 8, 2026 update  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-04-Carpet-Cleaning.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

June 8, 2026 update

## 1. Purpose

Aircraft carpets take more punishment than most people realize. Maintenance staff walk on them daily. Avionics technicians and pilots enter and exit for updates. Passengers track soil and moisture from shoes during boarding. The result: matted high-traffic areas, soil-loaded fibers, and stains from coffee, wine, food, oil, and grease. Most aircraft carpets are wool or wool blend — often custom-dyed, expensive to replace, and chemically sensitive. This SOP is built around protecting that fiber. The non-negotiable principle is: pH and PPM data drive every treatment decision. Never rely on visual assessment alone on wool. Wool that looks fine after cleaning can still hold alkaline residue that will brown the fiber within 24–48 hours. Wool that looks dry on the surface can still be wet underneath. pH/PPM readings and thermal verification are the truth. Carpet cleaning is performed after Interior Maintenance Cleaning (SOP #2) and after Leather Cleaning (SOP #3), if both are part of the job. Drying happens fast enough that leather conditioning can be done during the carpet drying window. Entry step cleaning (per SOP #2) happens AFTER carpet cleaning is complete.

## 3. Pre-Service Walkaround & CRM Documentation

Open the Shiny Jets CRM on your mobile device:
1. Navigate to the Customer → Aircraft → Job record.
2. Upload pre-service photos of each carpeted area — overall, high-traffic patterns, all stains close-up.
3. Document any pre-existing damage that won't come out (deep crushing, dye loss, prior repairs).
4. Confirm uploads completed. Then walk the cabin under normal lighting and note:
- High-traffic areas
- Visible stains and spills
- Previously treated spots
- Any signs of past wicking or browning (suggests alkaline residue left from prior service)

## 4. Pre-Inspection Protocol

4.1 UV / Black Light Scan
1. Darken the cabin as much as possible.
2. Scan the entire carpet surface with UV/black light.
3. Mark or note all stain locations — UV reveals protein stains (urine, food, body oils), certain dyes, and cleaning residues that are invisible in normal light.
4. Photograph any UV-revealed stains and add to the CRM record.

4.2 Baseline pH & PPM Testing (Control) This reading establishes what "clean" looks like on this specific carpet — your reference point for every other reading you take.
1. Select a clean, unstained area of carpet as your control.
2. Drop 3–5 drops of distilled water onto the area using the dropper.
3. Wait 30–60 seconds for the water to absorb into the fiber and pick up the carpet's resting chemistry.
4. Probe the YINMIK pH meter directly into the wet fiber. Hold steady until the reading stabilizes (10– 15 seconds).
5. Record pH and PPM — this is your baseline control reading. Expected baseline for clean wool: pH 4.5–7.0 | PPM near 0 (if distilled water was used).

4.3 Input Water Verification
1. Test your distilled water supply with the Bluelab OnePen.
2. pH should read 6.5–7.0 | PPM should read near 0.
3. If PPM is elevated, your water source has mineral contamination — do not use for testing or rinsing until resolved.

4.4 Per-Stain pH & PPM Testing For every stain identified in the UV scan and visual inspection:
1. Drop 3–5 drops of distilled water directly onto the stain.
2. Wait 30–60 seconds.
3. Probe the YINMIK directly into the wet fiber on the stain.
4. Record pH and PPM per stain location.
5. Compare to your control baseline.

Spot treatment application:
1. Apply the appropriate product to the stain area per product dilution instructions.
2. Allow appropriate dwell time per product directions — do not allow to dry on fiber.
3. Agitate gently with a soft brush if needed — do not scrub aggressively on wool.
4. Do not over-saturate — use minimum effective moisture.
5. Proceed immediately to extraction. Folex note: Folex pH is 9.0–10.0 — above the wool-safe alkaline threshold. If used for spot treatment, always follow immediately with a Fab Set or Wool Zone rinse and verify fiber pH returns to 5.5–7.0 before drying. Peroxide note: Avoid peroxide-based products (EncapuClean O2) on high-value custom-dyed wool with unknown dye history. Peroxide can strip or fade dye, particularly on bright reds, blues, and dark colors. Reserve for synthetic carpets or spot test extensively before use.

6.1 Wine / Red Stain Procedure Red wine and similar red-pigment stains on wool require a specialized two-part treatment:
1. Apply Pro's Choice CSS around the stain to protect the surrounding wool from spreading dye.
2. Apply Pro's Choice Stain Magic for Wool — Part A first, then Part B per product instructions.
3. Optional damp terry towel placed over the treated area to keep it moist during dwell.
4. Steam iron on lowest setting held over (not directly on) the damp terry for 3 seconds max, no more than 2 passes total. Heat accelerates the chemistry without scorching the fiber.
5. Extract the area thoroughly.
6. Rinse with 3:1 CSS dilution to neutralize.
7. Re-test pH — must read 5.5–7.0 before drying.

## 7. Full-Carpet Extraction Protocol

7.1 Pre-Spray (Full Carpet)
1. Mix Wool Perfect at 1:32 (4 oz per gallon hot water) — or 1:16 for heavy soil.
2. Apply evenly across the carpet — do not over-apply. The carpet should be damp, not wet.
3. Allow 3–5 minute dwell time.

4. Do not allow to dry before extraction.

7.2 Extraction Rinse Setup
1. Mix Wool Zone per product directions in the extractor tank.
2. Use warm or cold water for natural fibers — not hot. Hot water increases shrinkage and dye-bleed risk on wool.
3. Mixed Wool Zone target pH: 3.0–3.5. Spot-check with the Bluelab if you have any doubt about the mix.

7.3 Extract
1. Make overlapping passes with the extractor — do not rush.
2. Use slow, deliberate strokes to maximize water recovery.
3. Avoid excessive passes over the same area — more passes equals more moisture introduced.
4. For over-saturated areas, make additional dry passes (extractor running, no solution spraying) to pull maximum moisture out.

7.4 Post-Extraction pH & PPM Verification
1. Test the carpet fiber in treated areas by probing the YINMIK directly into the damp fiber: - Target fiber pH: 5.5–7.0 - Target fiber PPM: approaching your input water PPM
2. If fiber pH is still above 7.5 → apply Fab Set rinse and re-extract.
3. If fiber PPM is still very high → re-extract with additional Wool Zone passes.
4. Optional diagnostic: the waste water in the extractor tank can be sampled with the Bluelab if results aren't matching expectations — high PPM in waste water confirms soil is being removed; pH in the acidic range confirms the Wool Zone is doing its job.

7.5 Final Acid Rinse (If Needed)
- If any area tested above pH 7.5 after extraction, apply Fab Set (diluted 4–6 oz per gallon).
- Allow brief dwell, then extract.
- Re-test fiber pH with the YINMIK — must read 5.5–7.0 before proceeding to drying.

## 8. Encapsulation (Maintenance Cleaning Path)

For routine maintenance cleaning where deep extraction is not required:
1. Mix EncapuClean DS with Maxim at 1:32 (standard) or 1:16 (heavy soil).
2. Apply evenly with sprayer — light, consistent coverage.
3. Agitate with CRB, rotary brush, or bonnet machine.
4. Allow to dry completely — the encapsulating polymer crystallizes around soil as it dries.
5. Vacuum thoroughly once dry to remove encapsulated soil crystals.
6. Do not extract — encapsulation is a low-moisture process; extraction defeats the purpose.
7. Post-dry pH test: target 5.5–7.0.

EncapuClean DS with Maxim (pH 7.5–7.8) leaves a slightly alkaline residue. On wool, monitor for browning after drying. If browning occurs, mist with diluted Fab Set and allow to dry — this neutralizes residual alkalinity.

## 9. Drying Protocol

9.1 Setup Two scenarios depending on whether the dehumidifier fits in the cabin: A — Dehumidifier fits inside the cabin:
1. Seal the cabin entry door with painter's plastic and 2" (5 cm) tape to create a contained drying environment.
2. Run the dehumidifier drain hose out to a bucket outside the seal so the cabin doesn't re-introduce captured moisture.
3. Place directional fans inside the cabin to push fresh air in and move moisture toward the dehumidifier.
4. Target relative humidity below 50% inside the closed cabin. B — Dehumidifier too large for the cabin:
1. Position the dehumidifier outside the cabin, blowing into the entry.
2. Channel the airflow with painter's plastic so the dry air enters the cabin and pushes moist air out the same opening (lower vs upper).
3. Mats lined up in front of a carpet fan outside on painter's plastic to dry separately. ECS coordination: if ground power is available, coordinate with the flight crew or maintenance to run the aircraft ECS on a dry / low-humidity setting. This dramatically accelerates drying.

9.2 Drying Time Expectations

Condition                                                      Expected Dry Time

Normal post-extraction                                         2–4 hours

Over-saturated / wicking scenario                              4–8 hours

Tight weave wool (slower moisture release)                     Add 1–2 hours to above estimates

Wool absorbs and releases moisture 2–4x more slowly than synthetic carpet. Do not declare the job complete based on surface feel alone.

9.3 Thermal Verification (Required)
1. Attach the Topdon thermal imaging camera to the iPhone.
2. Scan the entire carpet surface systematically.
3. Cooler spots = moisture still present. Do not close up the aircraft until the thermal image is uniform.
4. Pay particular attention to: - Seams and edges (moisture migrates and pools) - Previously over-saturated areas - Areas under seat bases, table pedestals, and footwells - Piping - Cockpit footwells

5. Record video of the thermal scan to the CRM as drying verification.

9.4 Re-Dust Horizontal Surfaces After the dehumidifier and fans come out, re-dust all horizontal surfaces in the cabin with Quick Turn. The drying setup blows dust around the cabin — leaving the deep-cleaned carpet under a fresh film of dust fails the customer.

## 10. Final Verification & Documentation

10.1 Final pH & PPM Check Once carpet is confirmed dry by thermal scan:
1. Probe the YINMIK into 3–5 spots across the carpet (including any previously stained areas). Add a drop or two of distilled water if the fiber is fully dry — the probe needs moisture to read.
2. All readings must be within pH 5.5–7.0.
3. PPM should be at or near input water baseline.
4. If any area fails, treat with Fab Set, allow to dry, and re-test.

Critical Don'ts
- Don't rely on visual assessment alone on wool. pH and PPM readings drive every treatment decision.
- Don't use tap water for testing. Distilled water only. Tap water carries minerals that throw off readings and add PPM that doesn't belong there.
- Don't use hot water in the extractor on wool. Warm or cold only. Hot increases shrinkage and dye bleed risk.
- Don't leave a carpet at pH above 7.5 to dry. Alkaline residue causes browning within 24–48 hours.

- Don't use peroxide products (EncapuClean O2) on custom-dyed wool with unknown dye history. Spot test extensively first or skip.
- Don't use Folex without an immediate acid rinse afterward. Folex pH of 9.0–10.0 leaves alkaline residue that browns wool.
- Don't declare the carpet dry by surface feel alone. Wool releases moisture slowly. Thermal verification is required.
- Don't skip the re-dust step after pulling fans and the dehumidifier. The drying setup blows dust around the cabin.
- Don't over-saturate. Use the minimum effective moisture. More moisture means longer drying and more wicking risk.
- Don't rush extraction passes. Slow, deliberate strokes recover the most water.
- Don't skip the entry steps after carpet drying — entry step cleaning is the last step of the interior service package.
