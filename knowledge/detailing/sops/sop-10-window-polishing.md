# SOP #10 — Unpressurized Window Polishing

**Service:** Polishing of Unpressurized Aircraft Windows (Acrylic / Plexiglass)  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-10-Window-Polishing.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

## 1. Purpose

Unpressurized aircraft windows are typically acrylic (plexiglass) and develop scratches, hazing, and crazing from cleaning, environmental exposure, and contact with debris. Properly restored, the window returns to optical clarity with no distortion.

The procedure has two paths. Many windows can be restored with compounding alone. Deeper scratches require wet sanding first — 1000 grit, then 2000 grit, then back to compound. Heat must be controlled throughout because acrylic is heat-sensitive.

The non-negotiable rule of window polishing: whatever you do to one part of the window, you must do to the entire window. Sanding or compounding only one area creates an optical distortion that's visible in flight. Always work the full window.

## 2. Decision Tree

Inspect window │ ▼ Clean (Quick Turn + Bug Blitz mist, drag towel) │ ▼ Clean surrounding area above window │ ▼ Tape paint if necessary (skip if recessed PRC edge) │ ▼ TEST POLISH (ACA 500 + SPTA wool + 5" DA, ~45–60 sec) │ ├── Scratches gone → skip to Step 9 (Compound & Finish)

│ └── Scratches remain │ ▼ 1000 grit wet sand entire window │ ▼ Inspect — scratches out? │ ├── No → re-sand │ └── Yes │ ▼ 2000 grit wet sand entire window │ ▼ Compound & finish (Step 9)

## 4. Tools & Equipment

Sanding

- 1000 grit wet sandpaper — first sanding step when scratches are present
- 2000 grit wet sandpaper — second sanding step
- Foam interface pad — sits between backing plate and sandpaper. Keeps sanding flat and even.

Polishing

- Milwaukee DA polisher with 5" backing plate — main polishing tool
- 3" DA / Rupes 3" — for edges where the 5" throw is too big
- SPTA wool pad (DA-specific) — for ACA 500 compounding
- Rupes yellow foam pad — for ACA 520 finishing polish

### Pad cleaning

- Rag Company Tornador-style pad-cleaning tool, straight air only — between sections

Heat monitoring

- Thermal imaging camera — primary. Acrylic is heat-sensitive; monitor continuously.
- Laser-pointer infrared thermometer — fallback if no thermal camera

Towels

- Autofiber Motherfluffer — wipe-off compound and polish
- Multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber — initial cleaning, between-step wipes, protection application
- Suede applicator cloth — for hand-applying Fly Shiny Pro ceramic coating

### Other

- Fresh water in a spray bottle to keep sandpaper and window wet
- 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape — masking paint adjacent to the window (when not a recessed PRC edge)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access

## 5. PPE

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166.
- Hand protection: Nitrile gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1, minimum 6 mil. Required for ceramic coating application — skin oils contaminate the coating.
- Foot protection: Closed-toe non-marking work shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413.
- Clothing: Lint-free preferred for the ceramic coating step.

### Walkaround

Inspect each window:

- Scratch depth — surface scratches polish out, deeper scratches need sanding
- Crazing — fine spiderweb cracks in the acrylic. Crazing is a depth problem and may not fully come out.
- Edge condition — chips, lifting sealant, cracks at the perimeter
- Type of edge: recessed PRC edge (no tape needed) vs flush with paint (tape required)

## 7. Initial Cleaning

This step removes dust and bugs before any polishing tools touch the window. Doing this first protects the window from being scratched by debris dragged across it during compounding.

1. Spray Quick Turn (RTU) onto the window — light, even coverage.
2. Spray a few mists of Bug Blitz on top of the Quick Turn. Bug Blitz starts breaking down any bugs while the Quick Turn loosens dust.
3. Drag a clean multi-purpose microfiber towel across the window to lift the dust and any loosened bug residue. Don't scrub — drag the towel. Anything embedded in the dust scratches as soon as you start scrubbing.
4. Inspect — the window should be visibly cleaner, dust-free, with most bug residue lifted. Repeat if needed.

## 8. Surrounding Area Prep

Dust and debris that fall from above the window onto a wet sanding job is a fast way to scratch what you just sanded.

1. Clean the surrounding area above the window — sidewalls, panels, anything that could shed dust downward onto the work area as you sand or compound. Wipe with a multi-purpose microfiber.
2. Tape paint adjacent to the window with 2" (5 cm) painter's tape if the window edge is flush with paint. If the window is recessed and the edge is PRC sealant, no tape is needed.

## 9. Test Polish — Decide if Sanding Is Needed

Before reaching for sandpaper, find out if compound alone will remove the defects.

1. Set up the Milwaukee DA polisher with a 5" backing plate and an SPTA wool pad.
2. Apply ACA 500 to the window in a test section (not to the pad — applying to the pad cakes the wool fibers and cuts deeper holograms).
3. Set DA speed to one click below top speed.
4. Light pressure, cross-hatch passes, arm tempo about 1 inch (2.5 cm) per second.
5. Polish for about 45 seconds to 1 minute on the test section.
6. Wipe off the compound with the Autofiber Motherfluffer.
7. Inspect under good light: - Scratches gone → skip sanding. Continue compounding the entire window per Section 12. - Deep scratches remain → wet sanding is required. Continue to Section 10.

## 10. Wet Sanding — 1000 Grit

Whatever you do to one part of the window, you must do to the entire window. Sanding only one area creates a visible optical distortion. Sand the full window with 1000 grit first.

Setup
1. Sandpaper on a foam interface pad on the DA backing plate. The foam interface keeps the sandpaper conformed flat to the window surface — critical for distortion-free sanding.
2. Spray Quick Turn onto the sandpaper as the lubricant. Quick Turn keeps the sandpaper wet and the surface lubricated.
3. Spray the window with fresh water — the full sanding area needs to be wet.
4. DA speed: one click above the bottom setting. Wet sanding is a low-speed job.

Procedure
1. Break the window into sections that overlap with each other. The 5" pad can't cover an entire window in one pass; planned sections with overlap prevent skipped strips.
2. Sand each section evenly — no pressure. Let the DA's weight do the work. The sandpaper must stay flat to the surface throughout the pass.
3. Keep the sandpaper and window wet at all times. Re-spray Quick Turn and water as needed. Dry sandpaper grabs and gouges acrylic.
4. Leave a 2" (5 cm) gap between the sanding area and the edge of the window on your first jobs. Working too close to the edge with the 5" pad can run sandpaper into the paint or PRC. As skill builds, the gap can shrink. Experienced detailers can sand right up to the edge.
5. Sand each section enough to test whether the scratches are coming out — don't over-sand.

Inspect
1. Dry the window and wipe off all sanding residue with a clean multi-purpose microfiber.
2. Inspect under good light — scratches should be gone, replaced by a uniform fine matte finish from the 1000 grit.
3. If scratches remain, re-sand those areas — and remember the rule, you'll need to bring the rest of the window back into uniformity.

## 11. Wet Sanding — 2000 Grit

After 1000 grit, the window has a uniform matte finish that must be sanded back to clarity with progressively finer grits. 2000 grit removes the 1000 grit marks.

Setup Same as 1000 grit — foam interface pad, Quick Turn lubricant, water on the window, DA one click above bottom speed.

Procedure
1. Same section approach, same no-pressure, sandpaper-flat technique.
2. Leave a 1" (2.5 cm) gap from the edge with 2000 grit (smaller than the 1000 grit gap — because you're going to use the 3" DA on the edges next).
3. Use the 3" DA for the edges — the 5" throw is too big to do the edge cleanly without risking the paint or PRC. The 3" handles the perimeter band that the 5" couldn't reach.
4. Sand until all 1000 grit marks are removed and the window has a finer, more uniform matte finish.

Inspect
1. Dry the window and wipe off residue.
2. Confirm all 1000 grit marks are out. If any 1000 grit marks remain, more 2000 grit work on those areas — then bring the whole window back into uniformity.

## 12. Compound — ACA 500

Now restore optical clarity. ACA 500 with the SPTA wool pad on the DA cuts the 2000 grit haze back to a polished finish.

Edges First (3" DA) The 3" DA polishes the perimeter where the 5" can't safely reach.

1. SPTA wool pad on the 3" DA, ACA 500 applied to the window (not the pad).
2. 3 passes per edge section at the standard working speed (one click below top), light pressure, arm tempo about 1 inch (2.5 cm) per second.
3. Cover all edges of the window.

Body of the Window (5" DA)
1. SPTA wool pad on the 5" DA, ACA 500 applied to the window.
2. Break the window into sections (same as the sanding sections, overlapping).
3. 3 passes per section — light pressure, cross-hatch, ~45 seconds to 1 minute per pass.
4. Wipe with the Autofiber Motherfluffer between passes if needed; final wipe-off after the third pass.

Inspect The window should now look clear. There may still be very fine micro-marring from the wool pad — that's what the finish polish removes next.

## 13. Finish Polish — ACA 520 on Foam

ACA 520 with a foam pad removes the micro-marring left by the wool + ACA 500 step, bringing the window to full optical clarity.

1. Rupes yellow foam pad on the 5" DA.
2. Apply ACA 520 to the foam pad — 3–5 pea-sized drops.
3. 3 passes per section, same working speed, same light pressure, same arm tempo.
4. Wipe with the Autofiber Motherfluffer between passes if needed; final wipe-off after the third pass.

Final Inspect Under good light, the window should be clear with no scratches, no sanding marks, no holograms, no haze. Walk it under different lighting angles to be sure.

## 14. Heat Management

Acrylic windows are heat-sensitive. Excess heat from polishing can soften the surface and create distortion that won't come out.

The rule: don't let the window jump more than 20°F (11°C) over ambient.

- If it's 80°F (27°C) outside, the window should not exceed about 100°F (38°C) during any pass.
- Thermal camera on continuously — monitors heat buildup as you polish.
- Light pressure — pressure spikes heat fast.
- Maximum 45 seconds to 1 minute per section per pass — past that, compound has broken down and you're just adding heat.
- Spray water on the window between passes if it's running warm — water carries the heat off.
- Stop and let the window cool if temperature climbs into the warning range.

## 15. Pad Cleaning

- Wool pad — every section. Wool loads fast.
- Foam pad — every 2–3 sections.
- Rag Company Tornador-style cleaner, straight air only — no solution tank.
- Do not rake the wool pad on the DA. Compressed air is enough.

16.

### Protection

Once polishing is complete and the window is wiped clean, apply the chosen protection. Three options:

Fly Shiny Pro Ceramic Coating (Hand-Applied) The top-tier protection. Hand-applied with a suede applicator. Same general technique as Section 11 of SOP
#8 (Paint Protection), with a window-specific application direction rule.

1. Nitrile gloves on — skin oils contaminate the coating.
2. Suede applicator cloth wrapped on the applicator block.
3. 3–5 drops of Fly Shiny Pro onto the suede.
4. Application direction matters on windows: - Windscreen: apply top to bottom — the direction air flows over the surface in flight. This aligns any micro-pattern in the coating with airflow for better water shedding. - Side windows: apply top to bottom or bottom to top — straight vertical lines so water beads off uniformly.
5. Wipe in straight even passes in the chosen direction. No cross-hatch on windows for ceramic — the direction matters.
6. Watch for the flash / rainbow signal that the coating is ready to wipe off.
7. Two-towel wipe-off with plush ceramic wipe-off towels — first towel removes bulk, second towel levels.
8. Section by section across the window.

9. Cure window per the manufacturer's data sheet. Hangar the aircraft during cure.

Fly Shiny Air Guard Apply the same way you apply Air Guard on aircraft paint:

1. Apply Air Guard onto a damp multi-purpose microfiber.
2. Wipe onto the window in light overlapping passes.
3. Wipe dry with a separate clean microfiber before the product evaporates.

Fly Shiny Static Guard Apply the same way you apply Static Guard on aircraft paint:

1. Spray Static Guard onto a clean multi-purpose microfiber.
2. Wipe onto the window in light overlapping passes.
3. Let haze.
4. Buff off with a separate clean microfiber.

## 17. Quality Control

Walk each window under good light from multiple angles:

- No scratches, no sanding marks, no holograms, no haze
- No streaks from wipe-off
- Optical clarity uniform across the whole window — no distorted areas
- Tape (if used) removed cleanly; no residue on paint
- No protection product residue on adjacent paint or sealant
- All tools, pads, sandpaper, water bottles, towels, lighting, and tripod removed

Critical Don'ts

- Don't work only part of a window. Whatever you do to one section, do to the entire window. Partial work creates optical distortion that's visible in flight.
- Don't dry-sand acrylic. The window and sandpaper must stay wet at all times. Dry sandpaper grabs and gouges.
- Don't press hard. Light pressure on the DA at all times. Pressure adds heat, reduces orbital motion, and risks distortion.
- Don't let the window heat more than ~20°F (11°C) above ambient. Monitor the thermal camera. Stop if it climbs.
- Don't skip the test polish. Always try compound first to see if sanding is needed — many windows don't need it.
- Don't apply ACA 500 to the wool pad. Apply to the window. Compound on the pad cakes the wool fibers.
- Don't skip pad cleaning. Wool every section, foam every 2–3 sections.
- Don't rake the wool pad on the DA. Compressed air through the Tornador-style cleaner is enough.
- Don't work close to the edge with the 5" pad. Use the 3" for edges. Run sandpaper into the paint or PRC and you've created a much bigger problem.
- Don't clean pads inside the aircraft if working in a confined area. Step out and clean.
- Don't apply ceramic coating without nitrile gloves. Skin oils create voids.
- Don't apply ceramic across the entire window at once. Section by section — the coating flashes and cures fast.
- Don't apply protection on a warm window. Wait for ambient.
