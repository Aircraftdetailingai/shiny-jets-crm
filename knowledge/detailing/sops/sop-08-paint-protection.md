# SOP #8 — Paint Protection

**Service:** Paint Protection (Polishing Wax through Ceramic Coatings)  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-08-Paint-Protection.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

## 1. Purpose

Paint protection is the final step in the exterior service chain. After decontamination (SOP #5) and paint restoration (SOP #6), the paint is in its most vulnerable state — fully exposed, no waxes or sealants left on it. The protection layer is what determines how the paint holds up between services.

The Shiny Jets approach offers a five-tier protection ladder, from quick light wax to top-tier ceramic coating. The right choice depends on paint condition, customer expectation, durability requirement, and budget. The right choice is not always the most expensive one.

The five tiers, lightest to most durable:

1. 3D HD Speed — polishing wax (cleans + protects + adds gloss)

## 2. Fly Shiny Static Guard Spray Wax — quick spray wax

## 3. Fly Shiny Air Guard — inert spray protection

## 4. Permaguard — certified ceramic coating, hand-applied

## 5. Fly Shiny Pro — top-tier certified ceramic, machine-applied with a buffer

How to recommend the right tier:

- Start from the customer's goal — gloss enhancement, light protection, deep protection, or a long-term ceramic.
- Look at the paint condition — heavily restored paint deserves stronger protection so the restoration work isn't wasted.
- Look at the operating environment — frequent flying, harsh climates, and exposure to de-icing fluids favor stronger protection.
- Look at the service interval the operator wants — a customer who wants to forget about it for years is a ceramic candidate.

Document the chosen tier in the CRM job record.

## 4. Tools & Equipment

Application tools — by tier

- Spray bottles — Static Guard, Air Guard, and HD Speed (when applied by hand)
- Foam applicator pads — for HD Speed when applied by hand
- Milwaukee DA polisher with yellow foam pad — for HD Speed when applied by machine, and for Fly Shiny Pro application
- Ceramic-coating applicator blocks with suede applicator cloths — for Permaguard. The block gives controlled even pressure; the suede cloth holds product evenly and slides cleanly across the paint.
- Single-use suede cloths — wrap the applicator block. Fresh cloth per panel section.

Towels

- Autofiber Motherfluffer — buffing wax off paint (HD Speed, Static Guard, Air Guard). Same towel rules as SOP #6 — dense fibers, sewn-inside edges, dispersed hand pressure.
- Plush ceramic wipe-off towels — short-pile, edgeless towels specifically for the wipe-off step of ceramic coatings. Do not use the Motherfluffer for ceramic wipe-off — ceramics flash off too fast and a softer, shorter-pile towel is needed for the controlled level wipe.
- Multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber — general utility, alcohol wipe-down.

Inspection

- Isopropyl alcohol mixed 1:1 with water in a spray bottle — final pre-coating wipe-down. Strips any residue from the paint restoration polish so the ceramic bonds directly to bare paint.
- Headlamp — inspection under raking light during application and after wipe-off.

### Other

- Ladder tall enough for the aircraft
- Creeper for low panels
- 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape — masking decals, registration markings, brightwork, de-ice boots, lenses, composite panels, fresh paint under 30 days
- Nitrile gloves for ceramic application (skin oils contaminate the coating)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access

Pre-staging

- Tools and clothing completely free of brightwork polish residue and prior-job contamination before approaching the aircraft.
- Ceramic application area should be a shaded or hangared environment with stable temperature — direct sun cures ceramic too fast and traces of the application are locked in permanently.

## 5. PPE

PPE must meet recognized international occupational safety standards.

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166. Sprays and ceramic spit during application.
- Hand protection: Nitrile gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1, minimum 6 mil. Required for all ceramic application — skin oils on the applicator transfer to the paint and create voids in the coating.
- Foot protection: Closed-toe non-marking work shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413.
- Hearing protection: EN 352-1 or ANSI S3.19 rated if working near a running APU or adjacent running aircraft.
- Clothing: Long pants and long sleeves. Lint-free preferred for ceramic application — loose fibers from clothing can land on uncured ceramic and stay there.

## 7. Aircraft Preparation

1. Aircraft must already be decontaminated (SOP #5) and restored (SOP #6, if the chosen tier is ceramic or if oxidation was present). Protection over contamination locks the contamination under the protection — the customer will be unhappy.

2. Aircraft must be in shade or inside a hangar. Direct sun affects every tier — wax bonds poorly, sprays evaporate before they protect, and ceramics flash off in seconds and lock in streaks permanently. No exceptions for ceramic application.

3. Mask: - Decals, registration markings, placards - Lenses (nav lights, landing lights, beacons) -Brightwork / polished aluminum - De-ice boots - Composite panels not approved for the chosen product -Door seals and window seals - Any fresh paint repairs (under 30 days)

4. For ceramic application (Permaguard or Fly Shiny Pro): - Wipe the entire paint surface with the 1:1 isopropyl alcohol mix to strip any polish residue. The ceramic must bond directly to bare paint — any silicone or polish oils underneath will cause adhesion failure or voids. - Wipe section by section with the multi-purpose microfiber, refolding to fresh quarters as it loads. - Inspect under headlamp — the paint should look completely clean and dry with no shine from polish residue.

5. Stage the chosen product, applicators, and wipe-off towels in their working sequence.

6. Confirm hands, gloves, and clothing are free of brightwork polish residue.

## 8. Application — Tier 1: 3D HD Speed

3D HD Speed is a polishing wax — it does light cleaning while applying protection and gloss. Good fit for paint that's in OK shape and could use a light cut along with protection.

Procedure
1. Apply by machine (Milwaukee DA + yellow foam pad) for best results on a full aircraft. Hand application is fine for small sections.
2. 3–5 pea-sized drops on the foam pad. Spread with the pad off, then engage at a low-mid speed.
3. Work in sections of about 2'×2' (60×60 cm).

4. Pressure light, arm tempo about 1 inch (2.5 cm) per second.
5. Buff each section off with a clean Autofiber Motherfluffer once the product hazes.
6. Inspect under headlamp before moving to the next section.

HD Speed does not require a 1:1 alcohol pre-wipe; the polishing chemistry handles light contamination.

## 9. Application — Tier 2: Fly Shiny Static Guard Spray Wax

Static Guard is a quick spray wax — best when the paint is already in great shape and just needs a light protective refresh.

Procedure
1. Spray Static Guard onto a small section.
2. Spread with a clean Motherfluffer in light overlapping passes.
3. Flip the towel to a fresh quarter and buff dry to a uniform finish.
4. Work the full aircraft section by section.

No alcohol pre-wipe needed.

## 10. Application — Tier 3: Fly Shiny Air Guard

Air Guard is inert spray protection — stronger than Static Guard, intermediate before stepping into a ceramic. Good for customers who want longer-lasting protection without committing to a full ceramic service.

Procedure
1. Spray Air Guard onto a small section of paint.
2. Spread with a clean Motherfluffer in light overlapping passes.
3. Flip and buff dry with a fresh quarter or a fresh towel.
4. Work the full aircraft section by section.

No alcohol pre-wipe needed for Air Guard, but a clean restored surface gives the best result.

## 11. Application — Tier 4: Permaguard (Hand-Applied Ceramic)

Permaguard is a certified ceramic coating, hand-applied. Wipe-on, wipe-off — same general technique used in automotive ceramic coating.

This section teaches the hand-application technique used across most consumer ceramic coatings. The same technique applies to most wipe-on/wipe-off ceramics; the product-specific details (cure time, second-coat timing) come from the manufacturer's instructions.

Setup
1. Confirm the alcohol pre-wipe (Section 7, Step 4) is complete and the paint is fully dry.
2. Bring out fresh suede applicator cloths and a clean ceramic applicator block.
3. Wrap a suede cloth over the applicator block.
4. Nitrile gloves on. Skin oils on the applicator transfer to the paint and cause voids in the coating.

The Wipe-On / Wipe-Off Technique Ceramic coatings are applied section by section in small areas — typically 2 feet by 2 feet (60×60 cm) or smaller. The work pattern is what controls whether the coating goes on clean or streaks.

For each section:

1. Apply 3–5 drops of Permaguard directly onto the suede applicator cloth (not onto the paint).
2. Spread the coating in straight, even passes — first horizontal, then vertical. This cross-hatch pattern ensures even coverage on the entire section, leaving no holidays (missed spots). Light, even pressure on the applicator block.
3. Watch for the flash / rainbow: as Permaguard cures, it produces a faint rainbow / oily sheen on the surface. This is the signal that the coating has flashed and is ready to be wiped off.
4. Wipe off with a plush ceramic wipe-off towel — short-pile, edgeless. Two-towel method: - First towel: wipe off the bulk of the high spots with light pressure. This removes the excess coating before it cures hard. - Second towel (fresh): level the remaining film with a soft buff to a uniform finish.
5. Move to the next section and repeat. Each section is its own cycle: apply → spread → wait for flash → wipe off with two towels.

Critical Rules for Permaguard Application
- Work in small sections, never the whole panel at once. Permaguard cures fast — by the time you finish spreading across a large area, the first part of the area has already cured hard and won't wipe off cleanly.
- Refresh the suede cloth at least every 2–3 sections. A saturated applicator drags coating residue across new sections.
- Watch the flash carefully. Wiping too early leaves a wet film that won't level. Wiping too late means the coating has hardened and the wipe-off becomes a multi-pass scrub.
- Inspect each section under headlamp at a low angle before moving on. Any high spots, smears, or streaks must be wiped off immediately — once cured, they can only be removed by polishing the coating off and re-coating.

- Cure time: Permaguard typically requires multiple hours of cure before exposure to water or solvent. Reference the manufacturer's data sheet for the specific cure window. The aircraft should be hangared during this period.

Coverage and Second Coats Permaguard typically calls for 1 or 2 coats depending on the application. If a second coat is applied, the flash-off time between coats is per manufacturer instructions — usually 30 minutes to 2 hours.

## 12. Application — Tier 5: Fly Shiny Pro (Machine-Applied Ceramic)

Fly Shiny Pro is the top-tier certified ceramic coating. It is applied with a machine buffer rather than by hand, and uses approximately 1/3 the product of comparable ceramics because the buffer distributes it evenly across the paint at the micron scale.

The machine application is what makes Fly Shiny Pro's product economics work — applied by hand at the same coverage, the product cost would be the same as a comparable ceramic. The buffer is what changes the economics.

Setup
1. Confirm the alcohol pre-wipe (Section 7, Step 4) is complete and the paint is fully dry.
2. Milwaukee DA polisher with a fresh yellow foam pad dedicated to this product — no cross-contamination with polish or compound.
3. Pre-prime the pad lightly per the Fly Shiny Pro manufacturer's instructions.
4. Nitrile gloves on.

Application Fly Shiny Pro is applied section by section, like Permaguard, but with the buffer rather than a hand applicator.

For each section (typically 2 feet by 2 feet / 60×60 cm):

1. Apply approximately 1/3 the product you would apply for an equivalent area with a hand-applied ceramic. The buffer's even distribution means a little goes a long way. Per the manufacturer's data sheet, this is typically 5–8 small drops on the foam pad for a 2'×2' section.
2. Spread with the pad off — touch the pad to the paint, dab the product across the section evenly.
3. Engage the polisher at low-to-mid speed (one or two speeds above the lowest setting). Make slow, overlapping cross-hatch passes — horizontal then vertical — at an arm tempo of about 1 inch (2.5 cm) per second.
4. The pad spreads the coating into a thin, even film across the section. Watch for the same flash / rainbow indicator that Permaguard shows — same chemistry signal.
5. Wipe off with the two-towel method — first towel for bulk, second towel for level. Same as Permaguard.

6. Move to the next section.

Why the Buffer Method Works
- Even film thickness — the orbital motion of the DA distributes the coating into a controlled micron-scale film across the section. Hand application leaves variable film thickness, which means more product to cover the same area.
- No applicator drag marks — the buffer's continuous motion produces a perfectly even spread; hand applicators can leave faint streaks from the cloth fiber direction.
- Faster — a buffer covers a large aircraft significantly faster than hand application.

Critical Rules for Fly Shiny Pro Application
- Light pressure on the DA. Pressing reduces the orbital motion and creates uneven film thickness — the exact opposite of what the buffer method is for.
- Section-by-section. Same rule as Permaguard. Fly Shiny Pro cures fast.
- Refresh the foam pad regularly — every 2–3 sections, clean with compressed air via the Rag Company Tornador-style pad cleaner (SOP #6 method). Saturated pads spread coating unevenly.
- Use less product than feels right. The instinct is to apply more for better coverage. Apply less, trust the buffer, watch the result. If the section needs more, add a couple of drops — don't double the dose.
- Cure time and second-coat timing per the Fly Shiny Pro manufacturer's data sheet.

## 13. Inspection — All Tiers

After application of any tier:

1. Walk the aircraft under headlamp at a low angle (raking light).
2. Look for high spots (excess product, smears, streaks) on every panel. On ceramics, high spots must be wiped off immediately if still soft, or polished off and re-coated if cured.
3. Look for holidays (missed spots). On ceramics, these are easier to address — wipe a small amount of product onto the missed area and feather it into the surrounding cured coating.
4. Check decals, brightwork edges, lenses, and rivet lines for any product residue. Wipe with a multi-purpose microfiber.

## 14. Quality Control

Walk the aircraft and confirm:

- All masking and covers removed
- No product residue on decals, brightwork, lenses, rivet lines, or seams

- No high spots, streaks, or holidays on the protected surfaces
- For ceramic applications: the coating has had the manufacturer's specified flash time and is ready for the cure window
- Pitot tubes and static ports clear
- All tools, ladders, creepers, applicators, and used towels removed from around the aircraft

Critical Don'ts

- Don't apply any protection over contamination or oxidation. Decon and restore first; protection locks in whatever's underneath.
- Don't apply any tier in direct sunlight. Shade or hangar only. Direct sun affects every tier and is non-negotiable for ceramics.
- Don't skip the 1:1 alcohol pre-wipe before any ceramic. Polish residue under ceramic causes adhesion failure and voids.
- Don't apply ceramic without nitrile gloves. Skin oils transfer through the applicator and create voids.
- Don't apply ceramic across large areas at once. Section-by-section is the rule — cured high spots can only be removed by polishing.
- Don't wipe ceramic off with the Motherfluffer or any general microfiber. Use plush short-pile edgeless ceramic-specific wipe-off towels with the two-towel method.
- Don't reuse suede applicator cloths beyond 2–3 sections. Saturated applicators drag residue.
- Don't over-apply Fly Shiny Pro. About 1/3 the product compared to other ceramics is correct. Adding more product does not improve the result — it just leaves more for the wipe-off to deal with.
- Don't press hard on the DA during Fly Shiny Pro application. Pressing reduces orbital motion and creates uneven film thickness.
- Don't leave any tier exposed to weather before its manufacturer-specified cure window has passed. Hangar the aircraft until cure is complete.

- Don't approach the aircraft with brightwork polish residue on hands, gloves, clothing, or tools.
