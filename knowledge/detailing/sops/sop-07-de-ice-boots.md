## 1. Purpose

Pneumatic de-ice boots are critical flight safety equipment. They expand and contract repeatedly in flight to shed ice from leading edges, and they live in the harshest part of the airframe — direct UV exposure, airflow at altitude, fuel and oil contact, and constant flex cycling. Untreated, the rubber dries out, cracks, and fails years before it should.

The Shiny Jets de-ice boot service has three goals:

- Clean the rubber thoroughly without damaging it
- Restore and seal the rubber to extend service life and improve ice shedding
- Protect the finished surface with a maintenance protectant

The standard sequence is: Tape paint → Boot prep (strip old sealant) → Rinse → Tape over conductive cement → AgeMaster → Cure → Sealant → ICEX II → Maintenance Protectant.

"Prepping the boot" means stripping all the old sealant off the boot to get back to bare rubber. The boot prep is the active stripping product — it dissolves and lifts the previous sealant layer so AgeMaster can penetrate fresh rubber underneath. It is a prep product, not a wash — though it is rinsed off thoroughly after stripping.

The full restoration is a multi-day service because AgeMaster cures for 24 hours before sealant goes over it. Plan the service around that cure window.

## 2. Two Product Lines — Goodrich vs PBS

Two product lines exist. Middle steps (AgeMaster, ICEX II) and the procedure are identical. Only the prep and sealant differ.

Goodrich Product Line (Under Warranty) OEM-approved. Required while the aircraft is under Goodrich warranty.

- Prep: Goodrich ShineMaster Prep
- AgeMaster: Goodrich AgeMaster

- Sealant: Goodrich ShineMaster Sealant
- Ice retarder: ICEX II
- Maintenance: Goodrich Aerospace Protectant Spray

PBS Product Line (Out of Warranty or Owner-Preferred) PBS isn't labeled non-compliant with Goodrich boots — positioned as a compatible alternative. Safest used out-of-warranty or by owner request.

- Prep: PBS De-Ice Boot Prep
- AgeMaster: Goodrich AgeMaster
- Sealant: PBS Boot Sealant
- Ice retarder: ICEX II
- Maintenance: Goodrich Aerospace Protectant Spray or Fly Shiny Quick Turn concentrate

The maintenance protectants (Goodrich Aerospace Protectant Spray and Fly Shiny Quick Turn) work over either sealant — they're not tied to a specific product line.

Goodrich ShineMaster Prep off-gases ammonia — eye-watering, stinks up a hangar. Ventilate or use PBS prep in shared spaces.

Confirm warranty status before starting. Document the product line used in CRM.

## 4. Tools & Equipment

Application tools

- Magic eraser — for applying and agitating boot prep during the strip.
- Microfiber applicators (no internal liner) — for AgeMaster, sealant, ICEX II, and Quick Turn concentrate. Fresh applicator per product. Not the suede-faced ceramic-coating applicators.

Towels

- White cotton terry towels — 16"×16" (40×40 cm) — for the rinse-step wipe-down. Folded in half twice for four sides. Stock 10–20 per large boot.- Multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber — lint-pickup pass after rinse, final buffing, and maintenance buffing.

### Other

- Plenty of fresh water for rinsing
- Bucket for the cotton terry towels (filled with fresh water)
- Ladder tall enough for the wing leading edges
- Creeper for the lower surfaces of horizontal stabilizer boots
- 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape
- Acetone (small bottle) — for sealant cleanup with white cotton terry only (see Section 8 Step 9)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access
- Ventilation — required when using Goodrich ShineMaster Prep (ammonia)

## 5. PPE

PPE must meet recognized international occupational safety standards.

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166. When working the bottom side of the wing, boot prep and AgeMaster can drip down onto your face — glasses keep it out of your eyes. The products don't splatter aggressively, but gravity gets them onto you.
- Hand protection: Nitrile gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1, minimum 6 mil. Boot products stain skin and the chemistry is mildly aggressive.
- Respiratory awareness: Goodrich ShineMaster Prep off-gases ammonia that can make eyes water. In an enclosed hangar, ensure good airflow — open the doors, run a fan, or step out periodically. PBS prep doesn't have this problem.
- Foot protection: Closed-toe non-marking work shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413.
- Clothing: Long pants and long sleeves recommended. Boot products stain clothing.

### Walkaround

Walk each boot in the same direction every time. Pay particular attention to:

- Cracks, splits, or punctures in the rubber — these compromise pressure-holding and require repair or replacement, not just sealing. Notify the customer immediately if found.
- Edge lifting or peeling — the boot's bond to the leading edge is failing. Document but proceed with sealing only if the customer accepts that sealing does not fix bonding failures.
- Vortex generators (VGs) — small fins installed on or near the boot. VGs get cleaned and sealed just like the rest of the boot — they're not skipped. The critical rule is don't lift them. A lifted VG ruins the boot. The Citation Mustang's boots are notorious for this — if a VG lifts, the boot is essentially done. Apply prep, AgeMaster, sealant, and ICEX II up to and around the VGs, but use a light hand with the applicator near the VG base. Don't drag, don't pry, don't pull.
- Adjacent paint — the paint near a boot edge is what gets accidentally sealed if you're not careful. Tape protects the paint and makes a clean finish line at the boot edge.

Confirm with the customer or pilot:

- Goodrich warranty status (determines product line)
- Any boot repairs in the last 30 days (fresh patches need extra dwell time before chemistry can touch them)
- Whether the aircraft can be left for 24 hours of AgeMaster cure time between service days — if not, AgeMaster is skipped (see Section 9)

## 7. Aircraft Preparation

1. Boots must be intact and not actively leaking. If a boot is damaged, the work stops and the customer is notified. Sealing a damaged boot does not fix it.

2. Mask the painted surfaces adjacent to each boot with 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape. The tape line should follow the boot's edge exactly. Sealant on paint can be cleaned up with acetone on white cotton terry (Section 8 Step 9), but prevention is faster.

Conductive cement is NOT masked yet. It gets taped over after the boot prep strip and rinse — see Section 8 Step 2.5. Conductive cement masked from the start would block the prep from stripping the old sealant cleanly across the whole boot.

1. Work carefully around vortex generators if present. VGs are cleaned and sealed along with the boot. Do not lift them — a lifted VG ruins the boot (Citation Mustang boots are particularly vulnerable to this). Use a light hand near the VG bases throughout the entire procedure.

2. Aircraft positioned in shade or inside a hangar. Direct sunlight cures sealant too fast and causes streaking. Same rule as paint restoration — no direct sun on the work surface.

3. Position the ladder and creeper for the boots you'll be working.

4. Stage products by tool, kept physically separated: - Boot prep (product bottle) - Microfiber applicators (no liner) — one fresh applicator per product (AgeMaster, sealant, ICEX II, Quick Turn concentrate) - Bucket of fresh water with white cotton terry towels (16"×16") inside - Microfiber towel for the lint-pickup pass

## 8. Procedure — Step by Step

The standard sequence is identical for both product lines. The only difference is which prep and which sealant you reach for.

### Step 1 — Boot Prep (Strip Old Sealant)

The boot prep strips the old sealant layer off the boot. Goal: get back to bare rubber across the entire boot so AgeMaster can penetrate fresh rubber underneath.

Apply Goodrich ShineMaster Prep (Goodrich line) or PBS De-Ice Boot Prep (PBS line) with a magic eraser:

1. Pour or soak prep solution onto the magic eraser.
2. Work the boot with the eraser — apply the prep and agitate at the same time.
3. Keep the boot wet with prep solution the entire time — re-load the eraser as needed. The prep can't emulsify old sealant if it's dry on the surface.
4. Agitate for 15 minutes, keeping the boot wet throughout. That's typically enough dwell + agitation to emulsify and release all the old sealant.
5. Strip the entire boot surface, including over conductive cement areas. Conductive cement gets protected later (Step 2.5), after stripping is complete.
6. Avoid running prep onto adjacent paint — the masking protects it, but be deliberate with your aim.

### Step 2 — Rinse Boot Prep Off Completely

This step is critical. Any boot prep residue left on the boot will keep AgeMaster and sealant from laying down properly on application.

1. Rinse each boot thoroughly with fresh water.
2. Wipe with white cotton terry towels from the bucket — pull a towel out, wring it out until just barely dripping, wipe along the boot. Fold each 16"×16" (40×40 cm) terry in half twice for four working sides; flip to a fresh side as one loads, switch to a new towel when all four sides are loaded.
3. Keep wiping with fresh towel surfaces until a fresh side comes off the boot clean. That's the signal that all prep and emulsified old sealant has been removed. On large de-ice boots this commonly takes 10–20 towels.
4. Lint-pickup pass: one wipe across each boot with a clean microfiber towel saturated in fresh water. Picks up any lint the cotton terry shed.
5. Inspect — should look matte and uniform across all rubber surfaces. If any glossy patches remain, the strip wasn't complete on that area: run the boot prep again on those areas, then re-rinse with white terry to clean.
6. Let the boot air-dry. Don't towel-dry — and don't touch the boot. Finger oils, lint, or any contamination on the bare rubber will compromise AgeMaster and sealant adhesion. Just wait until the water has evaporated completely.

Step 2.5 — Tape Over Conductive Cement Conductive cement strips run along the boots on many installations (used to dissipate static electricity from the airframe). AgeMaster and sealant should not go on conductive cement — they look unsightly on the cement once cured. ICEX II is fine on conductive cement and won't discolor it.

Now that the boot is stripped and dry:

1. Identify the conductive cement on each boot. It typically appears as a dark, slightly textured band running along the boot.
2. Mask the conductive cement with 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape, covering the full width of each cement strip.
3. Press the tape firmly.
4. Inspect — every section of conductive cement should be covered before AgeMaster goes on.

### Step 3 — Apply AgeMaster

AgeMaster is the rubber restorer. It penetrates the rubber and re-plasticizes it — flexibility comes back, microcracking smooths out, and the rubber is prepared chemically for the sealant.

1. Use a fresh microfiber applicator (no liner). Do not reuse from any other product.
2. Saturate the applicator foam with AgeMaster — load it well so a single pass can cover the full length of the boot.
3. Apply AgeMaster in a thin, even coat. Work in full-length wipes.

4. Wait 15 minutes between coats.
5. Avoid pooling.
6. Inspect the coverage — should be uniform with no missed spots.

### Step 4 — Cure for 24 Hours

AgeMaster requires a 24-hour cure before sealant can be applied. Non-negotiable. Sealant over uncured AgeMaster causes adhesion failure.

During the cure window:

- Hangar the aircraft overnight so moisture doesn't accumulate on the curing boots.
- Don't wipe, buff, or rinse the boots during cure.
- Document cure start time in the CRM job record.

### Step 5 — Apply Sealant

After 24 hours, apply Goodrich ShineMaster Sealant (Goodrich line) or PBS Boot Sealant (PBS line):

1. Use a fresh microfiber applicator (no liner). Do not reuse from any previous step.
2. Saturate the foam so a single pass can run the length of the boot.
3. Apply the sealant in a thin, even coat with full-length wipes.
4. Coat counts: - 3 coats on the top side and leading edge of the boot - 2 coats on the bottom side - The uneven coat count is intentional — the top and leading edge see more wear, and this balances how the sealant wears down between services so it wears off evenly.
5. Don't exceed these counts. Too many coats and the sealant film cracks when the boot inflates.
6. Allow each coat to flash off per product instructions (typically 10–20 minutes) before the next.

### Step 6 — Apply ICEX II (Ice Retarder)

ICEX II is the ice retarder. It reduces the adhesion of ice to the boot surface so the de-ice system can shed ice more effectively.

1. Use a fresh microfiber applicator (no liner). Do not reuse from AgeMaster or sealant.
2. Saturate the foam with ICEX II.
3. Apply in thin even coats with full-length wipes. Multiple thin coats outperform one heavy coat.
4. Allow flash and cure time per product instructions before exposing the aircraft to weather.

### Step 7 — Maintenance Protectant

The maintenance protectant goes on as the final step of the restoration service and again at every subsequent maintenance visit.

Two options:

Goodrich Aerospace Protectant Spray — apply per the manufacturer's instructions. Light, even coverage across each boot.

Fly Shiny Quick Turn concentrate (Fly Shiny product, used over either Goodrich or PBS sealant):

1. Apply Quick Turn concentrate directly onto a fresh microfiber applicator (no liner) — concentrated, not diluted. Load the foam well.
2. Work the concentrate into the boot surface with overlapping full-length wipes.
3. Buff dry with a separate clean multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber towel until the surface is uniform and dry.
4. Extends sealant life on the boots.

### Step 8 — Remove Masking and Inspect

1. Remove the painter's tape from both the paint edges and the conductive cement strips. Pull at a low angle along the boot edge.
2. Inspect the boot edge line — the transition from boot to paint should be clean with no sealant on the paint and no missed coverage on the boot.
3. Inspect the conductive cement — should be free of AgeMaster and sealant. ICEX II on conductive cement is fine.
4. Inspect each vortex generator if present — confirm none have lifted during the service.
5. Wipe the painter's tape line edge with a multi-purpose microfiber if any tackiness remains.

### Step 9 — Removing Sealant From Paint (Repair Step)

Both Goodrich ShineMaster Sealant and PBS Boot Sealant can be removed off paint with acetone. Use this when sealant has ended up on the surrounding paint — either from a missed spot during this service or, more commonly, from a previous detailer who didn't mask properly.

1. Use a white cotton terry towel with acetone. White so any dye transfer is obvious; cotton terry because it holds the acetone and wipes the sealant off cleanly.
2. Do not use a microfiber towel with acetone. Acetone can dissolve the dyes used in colored microfibers and transfer that dye onto the paint.
3. Do not get acetone on conductive cement. Acetone will lift the cement edge. Keep it on the paint only.
4. Apply acetone to the terry towel (never directly to the paint).
5. Wipe the affected paint area with light pressure. Sealant comes off easily — no scrubbing needed.
6. Follow with a clean dry section of the terry towel to remove acetone residue.
7. Inspect the paint to confirm it's clean.

Document the situation in the CRM if you found this during a walkaround — note that a previous job left sealant on paint and that you cleaned it up. The customer should know.

## 9. When AgeMaster Must Be Skipped

If the aircraft is not available for the 24-hour AgeMaster cure window, the AgeMaster step is omitted. This is a compromise — the restoration is less complete — but it is acceptable when timing prevents the full multi-day service.

## 2. Rinse completely

3. Skip AgeMaster. Go directly to sealant.

## 4. Sealant

## 5. ICEX II

## 6. Maintenance protectant

Document in CRM that AgeMaster was skipped and the reason. The customer should know the boot's service life will be shorter than a full restoration.

When time allows on a future visit, the full procedure (with AgeMaster) should be performed.

## 10. Maintenance-Only Service (Between Full Restorations)

Between full restoration services, boots get a maintenance-only service to clean off bugs and contamination and refresh the protective film over the sealant.

Cleaning:

- Goodrich Aerospace Protectant Spray — clean the boots per the product instructions.
- Fly Shiny Quick Turn RTU (diluted 1 oz per 2.5 gallons of water) — used to remove bugs and surface contamination from the boots.

Optional extra protection (extends sealant life):

After cleaning, apply Fly Shiny Quick Turn concentrate to a microfiber applicator and work it into the boot, then buff dry with a multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber. This adds a fresh protective film over the sealant.

## 11. Quality Control

Walk the aircraft and confirm:

- All masking and tape removed (paint edge tape AND conductive cement tape)
- Conductive cement strips free of AgeMaster and sealant (ICEX II is fine on cement)

- No sealant, AgeMaster, ICEX II, or protectant residue on adjacent paint
- All boots have uniform coverage — no streaks, no missed spots, no pooling
- Vortex generators cleaned, sealed, and intact (not lifted)
- Edges of each boot show clean transition lines from boot to paint
- No painter's tape adhesive residue on paint
- All tools, ladders, creepers, and rinse water removed from around the aircraft

Critical Don'ts

- Don't use non-OEM products on an aircraft under Goodrich warranty. Document the product line used and confirm warranty status before starting.
- Don't apply sealant over uncured AgeMaster. The 24-hour cure window is non-negotiable when AgeMaster is used.
- Don't apply more than 3 coats of sealant on the top/leading edge or 2 coats on the bottom side. Thicker sealant film cracks when the boot inflates.
- Don't apply AgeMaster or sealant over conductive cement (it's unsightly on cement). Mask the cement strips with painter's tape after the boot prep strip and rinse, before AgeMaster. ICEX II on cement is fine.
- Don't apply AgeMaster, sealant, ICEX II, or protectant onto a wet or damp boot. Water under the chemistry causes adhesion failure.
- Don't leave boot prep residue on the boot. Fold each terry in half twice for four working sides; keep wiping until a fresh side comes off the boot clean. On large boots that's commonly 10–20 towels.
- Don't skip the microfiber lint-pickup pass after the cotton terry wipe-down. Cotton terry sheds lint as it loads — leftover lint shows up in the AgeMaster and sealant finish.
- Don't reuse microfiber applicators between products. Fresh applicator per product (AgeMaster, sealant, ICEX II, Quick Turn). Cross-contamination between chemistry layers compromises adhesion.

- Don't use suede-faced ceramic-coating applicators on boots. The internal liner blocks product from soaking through the foam — wrong for the full-length-wipe technique. Use the standard microfiber applicators without an internal liner.
- Don't lift a vortex generator. Work carefully around the bases with a light hand. A lifted VG ruins the boot (Citation Mustang boots especially).
- Don't work in direct sunlight. Sealant flashes off too fast and streaks.
- Don't use standard wash chemicals on boots. Anything petroleum-based or solvent-based damages the rubber.
- Don't apply product over visible boot damage (cracks, tears, lifting). Stop work and notify the customer — sealing does not fix structural boot damage.
- Don't use a microfiber towel with acetone to clean sealant off paint. Acetone can dissolve microfiber dyes and transfer the color onto the paint. Always use a white cotton terry towel with acetone.
- Don't get acetone on conductive cement. It will lift the cement edge. Acetone is for paint only.
- Don't rush the masking. Painter's tape on the boot edge is what makes the finish line look professional and protects the paint from chemistry.
