# SOP #2 — Dry Wash

**Service:** Maintenance Dry Wash (Full Exterior)  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Version:** 1.4  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-01-Dry-Wash.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

## 1. Purpose

The dry wash is the foundation of every exterior service we perform. It removes environmental contaminants, oil, exhaust soot, brake dust, and bugs from the aircraft without flooding water into pitot/static systems, overlapping panels, or hangar floors. Modern dry wash chemistry contains surfactants, lubricants, and drying agents that encapsulate dirt and leave a thin polymer layer behind — which extends the life of the paint between washes.

A dry wash is completed before any other exterior service (polishing, ceramic, brightwork, de-ice boots, windows).

## 3. Tools & Equipment

- Pressurized cleaning vessel (Detail Keg or equivalent), set to 120–150 psi

- Air compressor with hose (if vessel is air-fed)
- Creeper (for belly access)
- Ladder tall enough for specific aircraft
- Headlamp for inspection lighting
- Pitot tube covers
- Static port covers (or zero-residue painters tape if covers unavailable — locate every static port)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access (for photo uploads)

### Towels (color-coded)

- Dreadnaught XL twist-pile microfiber (1100 GSM) — main wash drying
- Dreadnaught blue 16"×16" — windows only (isolated)
- Cotton terry towels — oil removal and exhaust soot wipe-off
- Multi-purpose microfiber — hydraulic wipe-down, utility

## 4. PPE

PPE must meet recognized international occupational safety standards.

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166 (impact + splash rated)
- Hand protection: Nitrile chemical-resistant gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1 (chemical permeation) and EN ISO 374-5 (microorganisms). Disposable nitrile minimum 6 mil.
- Foot protection: Closed-toe, non-marking work shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413
- Hearing protection (when working near running APU or adjacent aircraft engines): EN 352-1 or ANSI S3.19 rated
- Clothing: Long pants, sleeves recommended when working with chemicals

## 5. Pre-Wash Walkaround (Mandatory)

### Shiny Jets CRM — Pre-Wash Photo Upload

Before the walkaround, open the Shiny Jets CRM on your mobile device:

1. Navigate to the Customer → select the Aircraft record (by tail number).
2. Open the active Job for today's service.
3. Upload pre-wash photos to the aircraft record: - Full exterior overview shots (nose, both sides, tail, top if accessible) - Close-up photos of every defect noted in the walkaround (dents, scratches, corrosion, chips, leaks, damaged fasteners) - Belly condition (oil/soot load) - Leading edges (bug load) - Brightwork condition - Window condition

4. Tag each photo with the affected location (e.g., "L wing leading edge," "belly aft of exhaust").
5. Confirm upload completed before moving to the walkaround.

These photos protect both the customer and the crew from disputes over pre-existing damage.

### Walkaround

Conducted before unloading any equipment. Walk the aircraft in the same direction every time so it becomes muscle memory.

### Inspect and document

- Dents, scratches, cracks, corrosion — fuselage, wings, tail, engines
- Damaged or missing fasteners on access panels
- Hydraulic leaks, bent struts, damaged wheels/tires
- Cracks/chips on windows and windshields
- Antenna damage or corrosion
- Fading, peeling, chipping paint
- Excessive oil or fluid leaks (stop work and notify POC immediately)

### Confirm closed and latched

- All cabin doors
- Luggage doors
- All windows that open
- Fuel caps

If damage is found: Stop. Notify pilot, owner, or mechanic (your designated POC) before starting work.

## 6. Aircraft Preparation

1. Install pitot tube covers.
2. Cover every static port. Some aircraft have multiple — confirm count with mechanic or maintenance manual if unsure.
3. Confirm doors and windows are closed.
4. Position pressurized vessel, creeper, and ladder for the side you'll start on.
5. Set out towels in dedicated bins (clean / dirty / windows isolated).

## 7. Procedure — Piston Aircraft

### Step 1 — Oil Removal

Spray Fly Shiny Oil Delete (oil remover) onto a folded cotton terry towel. Wipe oil off the belly and any oil-affected panels. You do not need to remove every trace — just break the oil free from the surface so the Carbon Blitz pass (Step 2) can finish the job.

For Skydrol (hydraulic fluid): Spray Oil Delete on a terry towel, wipe the affected area, then wipe dry with a fresh multi-purpose microfiber towel — the surface is clean and shiny, no further treatment needed. Skydrol areas are exempt from Step 6 neutralization.

### Step 2 — Exhaust Soot Removal (after Oil Delete on Engine Oil Areas)

Spray Fly Shiny Carbon Blitz (soot/carbon remover) onto a folded terry towel. Wipe soot-affected panels — typically the belly and aft of exhaust stacks. Re-apply Carbon Blitz to the towel as it loses effectiveness.

Why this step follows Oil Delete on piston aircraft: Oil Delete removes oil but does not remove exhaust soot. On piston aircraft, oil and exhaust soot are co-located on the belly and aft of the exhaust stacks, so the same area cleaned with Oil Delete must also be cleaned with Carbon Blitz to fully remove residue.

Goal: break soot free from the paint — streaks will be removed during the dry wash pass.

### Step 3 — Landing Gear (and Flaps for Bugs on Low-Wing Aircraft)

All aircraft: Complete landing gear cleaning per the Shiny Jets landing gear cleaning procedure (same method whether ramp or hangar).

Low-wing aircraft: Also pre-treat flaps for bug removal here. Heavy bug load on flaps should be loosened with Fly Shiny Bug Blitz (bug remover) since bugs are hard to remove during the wash itself.

High-wing aircraft: Skip the flap step — bugs will come off during the main wash pass.

### Step 4 — Main Dry Wash (top-down, systematic flow)

Starting point:

- Large aircraft: Tail, top-down.
- Small aircraft: Cockpit area, working around the engine to the other wing, then wash around the entire aircraft.

For each panel:

1. Apply Fly Shiny Quick Turn (dry wash) from the pressurized vessel in even, manageable coverage — only as much as you can wipe before it begins to evaporate.
2. Wipe with the Dreadnaught XL twist-pile microfiber, using both sides of the towel.
3. Work top-to-bottom, never the reverse.
4. Overlap wiping each pass by ~50%.

Painted leading edges: Spray Bug Blitz over the leading edge, then Quick Turn on top. The dry wash extends the working time so Bug Blitz can break bugs free without drying. Wipe gently with a damp Dreadnaught. Repeat passes (more pressure on subsequent passes only — the first pass removes any abrasive particles).

Polished aluminum leading edges: Do not use Bug Blitz. Light first pass with Quick Turn only. Re-spray, then dry. For stubborn bugs/stains, use Fly Shiny Maverick (sprayable metal polish) to remove bug etchings. Do not use Maverick on coated aluminum or on weeping edges.

De-ice boots: Quick Turn only — no other cleaners. Spray evenly, wipe in long gentle passes. Watch for vortex generators (Citation Mustang and others) — bumping them can damage the entire boot.

Bugs: On a properly executed main wash, bugs come off during this step on both high-wing (wings) and all aircraft (fuselage/nose/leading edges). Pre-treated flap bugs on low-wing aircraft are removed here.

### Step 5 — Windows (Final)

After the aircraft body is fully washed and overspray from dry wash risk is over, return to windows.

1. Spray Quick Turn evenly across each window.
2. Wipe with the dedicated blue 16"×16" (40×40 cm) Dreadnaught (windows only).
3. Wipe in the direction of airflow over the window.
4. Repeat until clear from all viewing angles.

The smaller dedicated towel reduces the risk of dragging contaminants like paint chips or PRC sealant from surrounding areas onto the glass/acrylic.

### Step 6 — Neutralize Chemical Treatment Areas (Engine Oil + Soot Areas Only)

Return to every panel where Oil Delete and Carbon Blitz were used together (the engine oil + soot areas — belly and aft of exhaust). Spray Quick Turn over the treated area and wipe clean with a fresh Dreadnaught XL.

Cleaner residue left on paint attracts dirt, slowly degrades the aircraft paint, and over time creates corrosion.

Skydrol/hydraulic areas are exempt — those were already finished with Oil Delete + dry microfiber in Step 1.

This step is not optional for engine oil + soot areas.

## 8. Procedure — Turboprop / Jet Aircraft

### Step 1 — Engine Oil

A dry cotton terry towel only is sufficient for engine oil on jets and turboprops. No chemical needed. Wipe oil-affected panels (typically around engine cowlings and aft of nacelles) with a dry terry towel before any other work.

### Step 2 — Hydraulic Oil (Skydrol)

For hydraulic oil leaks or Skydrol residue:

1. Spray Fly Shiny Oil Delete (oil remover) onto a folded cotton terry towel.
2. Wipe the hydraulic-affected area.
3. Wipe dry with a fresh multi-purpose microfiber towel — the surface is clean and shiny, no further treatment needed.

Skip this step if no hydraulic leaks are present.

### Step 3 — Landing Gear (and Flaps for Bugs on Low-Wing Aircraft)

All jets/turboprops: Complete landing gear cleaning per the Shiny Jets landing gear cleaning procedure (same method whether ramp or hangar).

Low-wing aircraft (most jets): Pre-treat flap leading edges with Bug Blitz if heavy bug load is present, so the main wash can remove them cleanly.

### Step 4 — Main Dry Wash

Same top-down, systematic flow as piston aircraft (Step 4 above). On jets, exhaust soot and bugs are removed during the main wash itself — no pre-treatment required for these contaminants under normal soot/bug loads.

For heavy thrust reverser soot on painted panels (the exception): saturate a Dreadnaught XL with Quick Turn, mist that towel with Carbon Blitz, wipe the soot-affected panel after spraying the panel with Quick Turn, then follow with fresh Quick Turn and a clean Dreadnaught to remove residue. Quick Turn neutralizes the Carbon Blitz–saturated towel and prevents potential staining.

Same special-surface rules apply (painted leading edges, polished aluminum, de-ice boots, vortex generators).

### Step 5 — Windows (Final)

Same as piston Step 5.

## 9. Quality Control Walkaround

Walk the aircraft in the same direction as the pre-wash. Verify:

- No streaks, missed panels, or pooled solution
- No towels, ladders, creeper, or tools left behind
- Pitot/static covers cleaned and reinstalled (or if tape was used, remove tape and wipe area clean)
- Windows clear and bug-free from all viewing angles
- Hangar floor wiped if any solution dripped

- All applicable neutralization complete (piston engine oil + soot areas neutralized with Quick Turn; Skydrol and jet hydraulic areas already finished with Oil Delete + dry microfiber)
- Any covers/plugs cleaned and reinstalled

### Shiny Jets CRM — Post-Wash Photo Upload

After QC is complete, open the Shiny Jets CRM on your mobile device:

1. Navigate to the same Customer → Aircraft → Job record from the pre-wash upload.
2. Upload post-wash photos to the aircraft record: - Full exterior overview shots from the same angles as pre-wash - Close-up beauty shots (leading edges, brightwork, windows, belly) - Any areas the customer specifically requested attention to
3. Mark the job complete: - If this is a standalone exterior maintenance wash: mark the job as complete.
- If this wash is a prep step for a larger job (decon, polish, ceramic, etc.): adjust the completion slider to the percent of total job hours completed.
4. Confirm uploads completed before leaving the aircraft.

Pre-wash and post-wash photos remain on the aircraft record permanently and become the customer's service history.

## 10. Critical Don'ts

- Don't start any service before the pre-wash CRM photo upload and walkaround.
- Don't leave the aircraft before the post-wash CRM upload is complete.
- Don't leave a hangar floor wet — it kills future invitations.
- Don't skip the neutralization step on engine oil + soot areas (piston).
- Don't scrub windows with pressure — drag gently.
- Don't use Bug Blitz on polished aluminum brightwork or de-ice boots.
- Don't bump vortex generators on de-ice boots.
- Don't wipe the chrome strut on landing gear — you'll strip the seal lubrication.
- Don't use a single towel for everything — color-code and isolate.
- Don't spray Quick Turn near electrical panels or open doors.
