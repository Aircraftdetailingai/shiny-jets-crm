# SOP #5 — Decontaminate Paint

**Service:** Paint Decontamination (Bonded Contamination Removal)  
**Author:** Shiny Jets  
**Source PDF:** `SOP-05-Decontaminate-Paint.pdf`

_Shop standard operating procedure for Detailing AI. Prefer this over generic advice when diagnosing or quoting this service._

## 1. Purpose

Aircraft paint accumulates contamination that washing cannot remove. Iron particles from brake dust and rail traffic, industrial fallout, tree sap, road tar, overspray, and bonded mineral deposits embed into the clearcoat over time. Left in place, this contamination:

- Causes oxidation and corrosion under the clearcoat
- Prevents proper bonding of polish, sealant, or ceramic coating
- Leaves the paint feeling rough to the touch instead of glass-smooth
- Reduces the aircraft's optical clarity and gloss

Paint decontamination removes everything bonded above and into the clearcoat so the paint is chemically and mechanically clean. It is the foundation for any paint correction, sealant, or ceramic coating service that follows.

Paint decontamination is performed AFTER a full wash — dry wash or maintenance wash — so loose contamination and oil residue (handled with Oil Delete during the wash) are already gone. Decon targets what the wash leaves behind: bonded above-surface contamination.

Only top surfaces are decontaminated. Top of the wings, fuselage top, horizontal stabilizer top, top of engine nacelles. These are the surfaces that collect the iron speckling, sap, fallout, and bonded mineral deposits gravity drives down onto them. Vertical and underside surfaces don't accumulate the same contamination and aren't part of the decon SOP.

The Shiny Jets approach uses a clay bar pad on the Milwaukee dual-action polisher, with Quick Turn as the clay lubricant, for general decontamination across the top surfaces. Overspray is removed separately using the red clay bar by hand — the red clay is more aggressive and is never used on the polisher. Hand-claying with the red clay is reserved specifically for overspray.

## 3. Tools & Equipment

Decon tools

- Milwaukee dual-action battery-powered polisher with clay bar pad sized to match the backing plate. Same Milwaukee unit used in SOP #4. The DA motion is safer on aircraft paint than rotary; the clay bar pad replaces hand-claying for a faster, more even result with less operator fatigue.
- Red clay bar — kept on hand for overspray removal. Used by hand only, never on the polisher.
- Pressurized vessel / spray bottle of Quick Turn — clay lubricant. Quick Turn already wets the panel during dry wash; for decon, additional Quick Turn is applied to keep the pad and surface lubricated throughout the pass.

Towels

- Dreadnaught XL twist-pile microfiber — wiping Quick Turn and clay residue off panels.
- Multi-purpose 16"×16" (40×40 cm) microfiber — between-panel wipe-downs, inspection wipes.

Inspection

- Thin plastic bag (sandwich bag, freezer bag) — slide your gloved hand inside and run it across the paint. The plastic bag magnifies the feeling of any contamination sitting on top of the paint — fallout, iron speckling, sap, or overspray. Smooth feel through the bag = decon complete on that section.
- Bare hand for overspray detection — running your hand directly over an overspray-affected area produces an audible rough scraping sound. The bag test confirms the feel; the hand test confirms overspray by sound.
- Headlamp — inspection under raking light.

### Protection

- Ladder tall enough for the aircraft
- Creeper for low panels

- 2" (5 cm) blue painter's tape — light use; mainly for keeping covers in place. Decon is paint-only by scope, not by tape — the detailer's discipline (pad stays on flat top paint, hand-clay around brightwork and boots) is what protects the rest.
- Pitot tube covers
- Static port covers (or zero-residue painters tape if rigid covers unavailable)
- Mobile device with Shiny Jets CRM access

Pre-staging

- All tools and the detailer's clothing must be completely free of brightwork polish residue, soot, oil, and prior-job contamination before approaching the aircraft. Brightwork polish residue on hands or pads will smear across freshly decontaminated paint and is much harder to remove than ordinary contamination.

## 4. PPE

PPE must meet recognized international occupational safety standards.

- Eye protection: Safety glasses meeting ANSI Z87.1 or EN 166. Quick Turn and decon residue spray can splash off the spinning pad.
- Hand protection: Nitrile gloves meeting EN ISO 374-1, minimum 6 mil. Protects the hands and also keeps brightwork polish residue, oil from skin, etc. from contaminating freshly decontaminated paint.
- Foot protection: Closed-toe non-marking work shoes meeting EN ISO 20345 or ASTM F2413.
- Hearing protection: EN 352-1 or ANSI S3.19 rated if working near a running APU or adjacent running aircraft.
- Clothing: Long pants and long sleeves recommended. Decon residue can stain.

### Walkaround

Walk the aircraft in the same direction every time. Focus on the top surfaces — these are what gets decontaminated.

Inspect and document the top surfaces:

- Iron speckling on horizontal surfaces (top of wings, fuselage spine, horizontal stabilizer, top of nacelles) — appears as brown or orange dots
- Tree sap, road tar, industrial fallout, bonded mineral deposits (water spots that survived the wash)
- Overspray — run your bare hand across suspect areas. Overspray makes an audible rough scraping sound under your hand. The plastic-bag test will also pick it up clearly. Document affected areas — these need red clay by hand, not the clay pad on the polisher.
- Pre-existing paint failures — clearcoat peeling, chips, scratches, oxidation. Decon won't fix these and may expose them more clearly.

Surfaces that are NOT decontaminated (must be masked or simply skipped):

- Vertical and underside surfaces — not part of this SOP
- Polished aluminum leading edges or brightwork — never decontaminated chemically or mechanically; has its own SOP
- De-ice boots — masked
- Composite panels (radomes, fairings, control surface skins) — many composite paint systems are sensitive; patch-test before any clay pad use, often skipped
- Recently repainted panels — paint that's less than 30 days old should not be decontaminated; clearcoat hasn't fully cured

Confirm with the customer or pilot:

- Pitot tubes and static ports are clear for masking
- No fresh paint repairs within 30 days
- Any composite panels or specialty finishes requiring special handling

Decon is always done fully — the depth and aggressiveness do not change based on what service comes next. The standard sequence is: decon → polish or compound (oxidation removal) → customer's choice of sealant, wax, or ceramic coating. Oxidation removal always comes immediately after decon, regardless of whether the customer is buying additional protection on top.

## 6. Aircraft Preparation

1. Aircraft must already be fully washed (SOP #1 Dry Wash or wet wash). Decon on a dirty aircraft drives loose grit into the paint during the clay pad pass and scratches every panel.

2. Mask only the pitot tubes and static ports. Everything else is handled by scope: we are decontaminating painted top surfaces only. The pad doesn't go anywhere else, so masking the rest of the aircraft isn't necessary. The detailer's discipline — keeping the pad on flat top paint and hand-claying around brightwork and de-ice boots — is what protects the other surfaces, not tape.

Areas the pad will not touch (no tape needed, just don't go there): - Brightwork / polished aluminum — hand-clay around (see Section 7) - De-ice boots — hand-clay around (see Section 7) - Decals, registration markings, placards - Lenses (nav lights, landing lights, beacons) - Composite panels with sensitive finishes (radomes, etc.) - Vertical and underside surfaces (outside the SOP scope) - Fresh paint under 30 days

1. Position the ladder and creeper for the panels you'll be working.

2. Pre-fill the Quick Turn spray bottle or pressurized vessel. The pad and the surface both need lubrication during the pass.

3. Load the clay bar pad onto the Milwaukee DA backing plate. Confirm the pad is sized to the backing plate — an undersized pad puts edge stress on the paint, an oversized pad reduces control.

4. Have the Dreadnaught towel and the multi-purpose microfiber towels staged within reach. Quick Turn residue on the panel after the pad pass is wiped immediately, while still wet.

5. Confirm hands, gloves, and clothing are free of brightwork polish residue and other prior-job contamination.

## 7. Decontamination Procedure — Clay Bar Pad on the Milwaukee DA

Work the top surfaces only — top of the wings, fuselage top, horizontal stabilizer top, top of engine nacelles. Move across each surface in sections, in the same direction as the wash. Never run the clay bar pad over rivets, overlapping panel edges, or from one panel onto an adjacent control surface (e.g., wing to aileron). The pad must stay on a single flat panel for the duration of each pass.

Per-Panel Procedure
1. Spray Quick Turn evenly across the panel as the clay lubricant. The surface must stay wet during the pad pass — dry contact is when scratching happens.

2. Apply additional Quick Turn directly to the clay bar pad to keep the pad face lubricated.

3. Set the Milwaukee DA to a low-to-mid speed setting. This is the same speed range used for paint polishing — the goal is mechanical contact, not aggressive cutting.

4. Pull the trigger and lay the pad flat against the panel. Light pressure only — the pad's weight plus the DA's motion does the work. Pressing into the panel does not improve decon results; it just risks marring.

5. Move slowly across the panel in overlapping passes (~50% overlap). Cover the full flat section of the panel.

6. Re-lubricate every 30–60 seconds with more Quick Turn on the surface and the pad. The moment the surface looks dry, stop and re-spray before continuing.

7. Wipe the panel immediately with the Dreadnaught towel while the Quick Turn residue is still wet. Dried Quick Turn residue is harder to remove and can leave streaks.

8. Inspect with the plastic-bag test: slide your gloved hand inside the bag, run it across the panel. If the paint feels glass-smooth through the bag, decon is complete on that section. If contamination is still felt, run the pad over that area again.

9. Inspect with the headlamp at a low angle, checking for any remaining iron speckling, sap spots, or visible bonded contamination on the top surface. Spot-treat as needed.

Areas the Pad Cannot Reach Tight areas, edges, fasteners, rivet lines, and any section where the pad would touch an adjacent panel are simply skipped. These aren't part of the decon scope. The pad stays on flat top-surface sections only — when the pad can't lay flat safely, move on.

Working Near Brightwork and De-Ice Boots — Hand-Clay With the Pad When decontaminating paint adjacent to polished aluminum brightwork or de-ice boots that are not being serviced this interval, do not run the polisher near those surfaces. Quick Turn solution and spray from the spinning pad will get on them, and if those surfaces aren't being serviced this interval, the residue is left behind on someone else's clean brightwork or boots.

For the paint adjacent to brightwork or de-ice boots:

1. Remove the clay bar pad from the polisher.
2. Hold the pad in your hand, lubricated with Quick Turn.
3. Hand-clay the paint in those border areas, working close to but not touching the brightwork or boot. Apply Quick Turn directly to the pad rather than spraying the surface, to control where the solution goes.
4. Wipe immediately with the Dreadnaught towel.

This is faster than re-masking those surfaces every job and keeps the workflow clean. The DA + pad goes back on as soon as you're back in open paint, away from brightwork and boots.

Overspray Removal — Red Clay Bar (Hand Only) Overspray is removed with the red clay bar by hand. The red clay is more aggressive than the standard clay pad and is never used on the polisher.

1. Use the red clay bar by hand only, on the affected overspray area.

2. Lubricate the surface and the red clay generously with Quick Turn.
3. Work in straight, light passes — no scrubbing. Fold the clay frequently to expose a clean surface.
4. Red clay will leave marring. This is expected. Any area worked with red clay must be compounded and polished afterward to remove the marring. Document the affected areas in CRM so the polish step is scoped correctly.

Overspray typically comes from a hangar paint job that drifted onto the aircraft, road work, or industrial sites. Detect it two ways: run your bare hand over the suspect area — overspray makes an audible rough scraping sound — and confirm with the plastic-bag test, where it feels distinctly rough.

Hand-claying is slow. The red clay bar is reserved specifically for overspray; it is not a default tool.

## 8. Don't Use the Pad on These Surfaces

The clay bar pad on the polisher is only used on flat top-surface painted panels with clean transitions. It must not be used on:

- Vertical or underside surfaces — outside the scope of decon
- Polished aluminum / brightwork — has its own polish process
- De-ice boots — masked, never decontaminated
- Composite panels with sensitive paint systems — patch-test first, often skipped entirely
- Across rivet lines — the pad can catch a raised rivet and damage either the rivet or the pad
- Across panel-to-control-surface transitions (wing → aileron, stabilizer → elevator) — even small gaps catch the pad
- Curved leading edges where the pad can't lay flat — these aren't part of the decon scope anyway
- Recently repainted panels (under 30 days) — clearcoat hasn't cured

When in doubt, mask the surface or skip it. The DA + clay bar pad is for the big flat top surfaces. Overspray is handled separately by hand with the red clay bar.

## 9. Final Wipe-Down and Inspection

After the entire aircraft has been decontaminated:

1. Walk the aircraft under headlamp, panel by panel.
2. Plastic-bag test every panel one final time. Note any spots that still feel rough — these typically need a second pad pass or a hand-clay treatment.
3. Inspect for any Quick Turn residue left on panels, decals, seams, or rivet lines. Wipe with the multi-purpose microfiber while damp.

4. Remove all masking tape and covers carefully. Inspect the masked edges for any tape residue and wipe clean.
5. Spot-check around brightwork edges for any pad contact marks. The polished aluminum stays masked through decon for a reason — if the mask shifted, document it.

The decontaminated paint is now ready for the next service:

- Light/Medium/Heavy Oxidation Removal (polish or compound)

That's the only service that directly follows decon. Sealants, ceramic coatings, and other protective layers come after oxidation removal, not directly after decon.

Decon by itself doesn't provide protection. The paint is now in its most vulnerable state — fully exposed, no waxes or sealants left on it. Move to oxidation removal the same day, or return the aircraft to the hangar covered until oxidation removal can be done.

## 10. Quality Control

Walk the aircraft and confirm:

- All masking and covers removed
- No Quick Turn residue on decals, brightwork mask edges, lenses, or rivet lines
- Plastic-bag test passes on all decontaminated panels — paint feels glass-smooth
- Pre-existing paint defects (scratches, oxidation, chips) documented in CRM are still documented post-decon
- No new marring introduced by the decon process — if any panel shows marring, document and notify the customer
- Pitot tubes and static ports uncovered and confirmed clear
- All tools, ladders, creepers, and hoses removed from around the aircraft

Shiny Jets CRM — Post-Service Documentation After QC is complete:

1. Navigate to the same Customer → Aircraft → Job record.
2. Upload only what's documentable: - Photos of any previously rust-stained or iron-speckled areas showing the spots are gone - Video of your hand running over a previously overspray-affected area confirming the rough scraping sound is gone — proof that the overspray is off - Any new paint defects exposed by decon that weren't visible before (these were under contamination and the customer needs to know)
3. Don't force post-service photos for invisible contamination. Most of the decon work is felt through the bag, not seen.
4. Note in the job record: - Whether red clay was used on any panels (and which panels — these will need polish afterward) - Any panels that were skipped (composite, fresh paint, etc.) and why

5. Mark the job complete: - Standalone paint decontamination: mark complete. - Part of a paint correction or ceramic coating service: adjust the completion slider to the percent of total job hours completed.
6. Confirm uploads completed.

Critical Don'ts

- Don't decontaminate paint on a dirty aircraft. Wash first, decon second.
- Don't run the clay bar pad over rivets, overlapping panel seams, or from one panel onto an adjacent control surface. Stay on flat sections only.
- Don't let the pad and surface go dry. Re-lubricate with Quick Turn continuously.
- Don't press hard. Light pressure + pad weight + DA motion is the technique. Pressing causes marring.
- Don't use red clay on the polisher. Red clay is hand-only.
- Don't decontaminate polished aluminum, de-ice boots, or fresh paint (under 30 days). Mask them.
- Don't decontaminate composite panels without a patch test. Many composite paint systems are sensitive.
- Don't approach the aircraft with brightwork polish residue on hands, gloves, clothing, or tools. Decontamination exposes raw paint that residue will stain.
- Don't leave decontaminated paint exposed overnight without protection. The paint is in its most vulnerable state right after decon — move to oxidation removal (polish or compound) the same day or cover the aircraft.
- Don't skip documenting panels that required red clay. Those panels must be compounded and polished afterward to remove red-clay marring, and that needs to be in the scope of work.
