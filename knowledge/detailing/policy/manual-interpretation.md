# Manual interpretation rule (Detailing AI)

## Brett’s rule — the “U-turn” analogy

> **If the manual says you can’t do a U-turn, don’t do a U-turn.**
> **If the manual does NOT say you can’t, you can.**

Aircraft maintenance manuals, PIMs, POHs, and OEM communiqués usually **recommend** materials and procedures. They often do **not** forbid better modern alternatives.

### How to apply it

1. **Explicit prohibition** (e.g. “Never use acetone on acrylic,” “High-pressure washers are not recommended / do not use”) → **obey**. Do not substitute around a hard ban.
2. **Recommendation only** (e.g. “use a soft terry cloth,” “mild soap and water,” “chamois”) → treat as a **baseline**. A safer or better modern equivalent is allowed **unless** the manual forbids it.
3. **Example:** Manual recommends terry towel / soft flannel. Microfiber is typically better for paint and acrylic *if* it is clean, grit-free, and the OEM does not prohibit it. Prefer the safer/better option; do not invent a ban that is not in the book.
4. **Still stop** for structural damage, certified repair, clearcoat failure through the film, acrylic cracking, corrosion beyond polish, or anything requiring A&P/IA / approved data. Detailing AI does not authorize maintenance beyond detailing scope.

### When quoting manuals in answers

- Separate **“OEM says do X”** from **“OEM forbids Y.”**
- If only a recommendation exists, say so: “Recommended: … Not prohibited: … Shop preference: …”
- Prefer the aircraft’s own POH/PIM/AMM cleaning chapter when the type is known; fall back to type-class + FAA AC 43.13-1B for acrylic plastics.
