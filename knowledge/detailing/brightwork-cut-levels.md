# Brightwork — Cut Levels & Expectations

**Common ask:** "Can you make the leading edges / engines / exhaust look new?"

## What you're looking at
Brightwork (polished aluminum / stainless leading edges, spinners, exhaust, sometimes door surrounds) oxidizes and pits. Cut level is how aggressive you go to restore mirror.

## Clarify before you recommend
- Alloy / finish type if known (clad aluminum vs. stainless)?
- Pitting depth — light haze, orange peel oxidation, or cratered pits?
- Last polish date and who did it?
- Flight schedule — how many days until next flight / wash cycle?
- Customer goal: show shine for photos, or durable working polish?

## Cut level guidance (shop language)
- **Level 1 — Maintain:** Light polish / finish pass. Good for regularly maintained jets.
- **Level 2 — Restore:** Medium cut + polish. Typical for annual or neglected brightwork with haze.
- **Level 3 — Deep cut:** Heavy compound work, more hours, risk of uneven panels if rushed. Quote conservatively.
- **Beyond detailing:** Deep pitting / corrosion that needs blending or replacement — coordinate with maintenance, don't fake it with polish.

## Typical service alignment
Pair brightwork with exterior wash/detail so runoff and polish residue are managed cleanly. Ceramic or metal sealant after polish extends the look between visits.

## Brett's notes
Under-quoting brightwork is the #1 way to lose money on a "simple" exterior. Measure linear feet / surfaces, not vibes. Ask for photos of leading edges and engine inlets before locking the number.
